The true-cost worksheet for a cheap Japanese house

A ¥3,000,000 akiya is never a ¥3,000,000 purchase. Fill in the right-hand column for the house you are looking at — the example column shows a typical ¥3,000,000 rural listing so you can see how the numbers stack up. Free, sent to your inbox.

What's inside

1What you pay once, at purchase — agent commission (¥165,000 at the legal maximum on ¥3,000,000), registration taxes, acquisition tax, stamp duty and the tax settlement. Example total: ≈ ¥3,250,000 – ¥3,620,000, before any renovation.
2What you pay every year — fixed-asset tax, septic tank, fire + earthquake insurance, utility basic charges and a maintenance reserve. Typical range: ≈ ¥230,000 – ¥520,000 / year.
3Renovation ranges that decide the deal — roof ¥500,000–1,500,000, bathroom ¥600,000–1,200,000, kitchen ¥500,000–1,500,000, septic replacement ¥1,000,000+, seismic retrofit ¥1,000,000–2,500,000, clean-out and pest treatment ¥100,000–500,000.
4A blank column for your own listing — the same tables with space to fill in, so the worksheet becomes your numbers, not just an example.

All figures are planning estimates in yen, based on the standard formulas and ranges noted in the sheet. Your exact figures depend on the official assessed value (固定資産税評価額), the municipality, and the property's condition.
