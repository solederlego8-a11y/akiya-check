About Akiya Check

Akiya Check turns a Japanese property listing into an English risk report built from Japan's official zoning, hazard and transaction datasets. This page is the long version of "who are you and where does the data come from" — written so you can judge for yourself how much to trust a report.

Who runs it

Akiya Check is operated by Follows, a small independent publisher based in Japan that also runs Japanese-language consumer sites. We are not a real-estate agent, broker, or developer, we do not list properties, and we receive no referral fees from sellers or agents. The only revenue from this site is the report price. Legal identity and business details are on the 特定商取引法に基づく表記 (the disclosure required under Japan's Act on Specified Commercial Transactions).

The service started in September 2026. We say this plainly because "500+ reports delivered" style badges are easy to fake and we would rather earn trust by showing our method.

How a score is decided

Each item gets one of four labels. The rules are deterministic — no language model decides whether a lot is 再建築不可.

  • OK — the source states a condition with no known downside (e.g. freehold, public sewer, not inside a designated hazard zone).
  • CAUTION — a condition that changes cost or effort but is common and manageable (e.g. septic tank, propane, pre-1981 construction, setback required, akiya-bank conditions).
  • RISK — a condition that limits what you can do with the property or its resale (e.g. rebuilding prohibited, urbanization control area, disclosure matters present, inside a special landslide zone).
  • UNKNOWN — the listing did not say. Unknown items are not counted as OK, and each one comes with the question to send the agent.

The overall letter (A–E) counts RISK and CAUTION items with fixed weights. It is a triage grade, not a valuation. Two houses with the same letter can be very different in practice; the point of the grade is to tell you which items to ask about first.

What the report cannot tell you

  • The physical condition of the building — termites, roof, structure, wiring. Nobody can inspect a roof from a URL. Budget for an on-site inspection.
  • Micro-topography. A lot 2 m above its neighbour behaves differently in a flood; the datasets are area designations.
  • Whether the municipality will actually issue a permit for your plan. We report the zoning; the building department decides.
  • Anything the listing hides. If a listing omits 告知事項, the report cannot know. That is why UNKNOWN items exist.
  • Legal, tax, or brokerage advice. The report is desk research; contracts, registration and tax need licensed professionals.

Refund rule

If a report states a fact as OK / CAUTION / RISK and the official dataset or the listing says otherwise, we refund the full price. If we cannot produce a report from the data available, we refund the full price. UNKNOWN items are explicitly not claims and are not covered. Details are in the terms.

How the guides are written

Every guide on the Blog & Guides page cites the law or dataset it relies on (Building Standards Act articles, MLIT and GSI portals, municipal programme pages). We do not publish testimonials, invented case studies, or "expert" bylines. When a guide gives a cost range, it is a range from public sources, not a measurement we took.
