Japan Hazard Map in English: open the official flood, landslide, tsunami and liquefaction maps for any address

What this does: type a Japanese address (or paste coordinates) and this page gives you one-click links that open Japan's official hazard map, the Geospatial Information Authority's map, and MLIT's Real Estate Information Library already centered on that spot, with the right hazard layer switched on. Those three government sites are excellent, but their menus and legends are in Japanese. Below the tool you will find an English guide to what each layer means, how to read its colors, and what the Japanese legend labels say.

Independence note: the three map services below are run by the Japanese government — the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) and the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (GSI). Akiya Check is a separate, private information service and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to MLIT or GSI. This page only builds links to their public sites; the maps and data belong to them.

If the address search fails: copy coordinates from Google Maps

The address lookup uses the address search that GSI built for its own map site. It runs in your browser, and GSI has said that these search functions are intended mainly for its own map and may change or stop without notice. It also only understands Japanese addresses. If you see an error, or the result lands in the wrong town, use coordinates instead — they are exact and work every time:

  1. On a computer: open Google Maps, find the house (search the address from the listing, or zoom in on the photo landmarks), right-click the exact building, and click the two numbers at the top of the menu (for example 36.23799, 137.97145). They are copied to your clipboard.
  2. On a phone: in the Google Maps app, press and hold on the building to drop a pin. The coordinates appear in the search box or the pin card; tap to copy them.
  3. Paste them into the box above. A Google Maps link that contains @36.23799,137.97145 also works — we read the numbers out of it.

Coordinates should be latitude first (about 24 to 46 in Japan) and longitude second (about 123 to 154). If you paste them the other way round, the tool swaps them for you.

Map 1: Kasaneru Hazard Map (重ねるハザードマップ) — what each layer means

The Hazard Map Portal Site is run by MLIT's Water and Disaster Management Bureau together with GSI. Its "Kasaneru" (overlay) map gathers hazard data made by national and prefectural agencies into one national map. Two points from its terms of use matter for buyers. First, the portal says its mesh size, drawing method and legends may differ from the hazard maps that the agencies themselves publish, and some places have no data yet. For the latest and most detailed version it points you to the municipality's own hazard map, which you can find through the portal's わがまちハザードマップ (Wagamachi, "my town") link list. Second, it notes that real-estate agents cannot use the overlay map for the legally required pre-contract explanation. So use it to screen, and ask for the municipal map at the important-matters explanation.

Tip: click any point on the map and the portal shows the hazards at that point in a Japanese text pop-up. A browser translator handles those pop-ups well, but check the numbers against the legend below.

Flood (洪水・内水): the 想定最大規模 depth bands

The flood button turns on the 洪水浸水想定区域（想定最大規模） layer: the area expected to flood if a river overflows in the largest rainfall the river authority assumes (想定最大規模, "assumed maximum scale"). It is a worst-case planning scenario, not an every-year event. The same button also shows two smaller flood layers where they exist: 内水 (inland flooding, when drains and small channels back up in heavy rain) and ため池 (flooding if an irrigation pond breaks). The depth colors follow the national legend; the plain-English meaning in the third column comes from the portal's own picture key.

The color swatches on this page are approximations of the portal's colors and are only a reading aid; trust the colors and legend on the official map. What to note: the band at the house itself (not the street), and whether the lot is on the edge of a colored area. Under the portal's "すべての情報から選択" (choose from all information) menu you can also add 浸水継続時間 (how long the water is expected to stay) and 家屋倒壊等氾濫想定区域 (zones next to rivers where the flow or bank erosion could knock houses down) — the second one is a serious flag for a wooden house.

Landslide (土砂災害): yellow zones and red zones

The landslide button turns on the 土砂災害警戒区域等 layers, which prefectures designate under the Act on Sediment Disaster Countermeasures. There are three phenomena, each in its own colors, and two levels:

JapaneseEnglishColors on the map
急傾斜地の崩壊Steep-slope failure (a slope collapsing onto houses) 警戒区域 · 特別警戒区域
土石流Debris flow (mud and rock rushing down a stream valley) 警戒区域 · 特別警戒区域
地すべりLandslide (a large block of ground slowly sliding) 警戒区域 · 特別警戒区域
  • 土砂災害警戒区域 — "Sediment Disaster Warning Area" (the "yellow zone"): residents' lives could be at risk in a sediment disaster. It mainly drives warning and evacuation planning.
  • 土砂災害特別警戒区域 — "Special Warning Area" (the "red zone"): buildings could be damaged and people seriously hurt. Certain development needs permission and new living spaces face structural rules, which can make rebuilding expensive.
  • 指定済 / 指定予定: "designated" areas are filled; "planned" areas (surveyed but not yet designated) are drawn with a dashed blue border. Treat a planned zone as a zone.

Tsunami (津波): inundation depth

The tsunami button turns on 津波浸水想定, the maximum-scale tsunami inundation that each coastal prefecture sets under the Act on Building Communities Resilient to Tsunamis. The legend uses the same color family as the flood map, but some prefectures add finer bands near the bottom: 〜0.3m (up to 0.3 m), 〜0.5m, 0.5m〜1m, 0.5m〜3m, 3m〜5m, 5m〜10m, 10m〜20m and 20m〜. Even 0.3–1 m of moving tsunami water is dangerous to people on foot, so for a coastal house also check how far it is to higher ground — the evacuation-site symbols (指定緊急避難場所) can be added from the portal's menu.

Liquefaction (液状化): the 5-level tendency map

The liquefaction button shows 地形区分に基づく液状化の発生傾向図, a national map that ranks each roughly 250 m mesh by how easily its type of ground has liquefied in past earthquakes. It is a screening map, not a prediction for one lot. The legend runs from 強 (strong tendency) at the top to 弱 (weak) at the bottom:

ColorJapanese legendEnglish
埋立地や旧河道などStrongest: reclaimed land, former river channels, etc.
干拓地や自然堤防などDrained lakebed/sea land (polders), natural levees, etc.
緩勾配の谷底低地、緩勾配の扇状地などGently sloping valley floors and alluvial fans
急勾配の谷底低地、急勾配の扇状地などSteeper valley floors and alluvial fans
山地や丘陵などWeakest: mountains and hills

Liquefaction is not part of the flood-map explanation an agent must give before a contract, so this layer is one you have to check yourself. Our longer guide explains what to do with the result: how to check liquefaction risk for a Japanese house.

Map 2: GSI Maps (地理院地図) — reading the ground itself

GSI Maps is the national topographic map. It does not draw hazard zones, but it shows the shape and history of the land, which explains why a lot is on the hazard map.

  • Standard map + elevation: the bar at the bottom of the screen shows 標高 (elevation in metres) at the crosshair. Compare the house with the nearest river and the surrounding streets: a lot a metre or two lower than its neighbours collects water.
  • 治水地形分類図 (landform classification for flood control): GSI built this map for river basins managed by the national and prefectural governments, mainly the plains of 109 major river systems, so it is blank in many rural and mountain areas. Where it exists, look for 旧河道 (former river channel), 後背湿地 (back marsh — low wet ground behind a levee), 自然堤防 (natural levee — slightly higher, usually safer) and 扇状地 (alluvial fan). A house on a former channel or back marsh is more exposed to flooding and liquefaction. See GSI's explanation (Japanese).
  • Aerial photos 1961–1969: shows what was there before the house. Rice paddies, a pond or a river bend under today's housing lot are a reason to look harder at ground conditions. Coverage varies; if the layer is blank, try the other periods from the 写真 (photos) menu.

What the maps can't tell you

Hazard maps answer one question — what nature might do to this spot. They say nothing about whether you are allowed to rebuild the house if it burns down (再建築不可), whether the lot touches a legal road, whether there is a septic tank, a leasehold or a disclosure matter in the listing, or whether the asking price is in line with recent sales nearby. Those facts sit in the listing text, the zoning layers and the transaction records, and you have to cross-check them one by one. FAQ

Is there an official English version of Japan's hazard map?

The Kasaneru Hazard Map and its legends are in Japanese, and the municipal hazard maps are usually Japanese PDFs. Some cities publish English or multilingual hazard maps, so search the municipality's website as well. This page gives you English explanations of the national map layers and links straight to the right spot, but the maps themselves stay in Japanese.

Why did my English address not work?

The address search this tool uses is GSI's Japanese address search, and in our tests it returned no results for romanized addresses. Copy the Japanese address from the listing (the 所在地 line), or paste latitude and longitude from Google Maps. Coordinates are also more precise than a partial address, which may only match the town centre.

Does a blank area on the hazard map mean the house is safe?

No. A blank area can mean the modeled risk is low, or that no data has been prepared for that hazard there yet; the portal itself says some places are not yet mapped. Hazard maps are also based on assumed scenarios, and real events can exceed them. Check each layer separately and confirm with the municipality's own hazard map.

Is this tool free, and does Akiya Check store what I type?

The tool is free and runs in your browser. The address you type is sent directly from your browser to GSI's address search and reverse-geocoding services to find the coordinates and municipality, and the map links open the government sites in a new tab. This page does not save the address. Our analytics record page visits and button clicks, not the address text.

Can I rely on these maps instead of the agent's explanation before signing?

No. Since 28 August 2020 the agent must show you the property's location on the municipal flood hazard maps during the important-matters explanation (重要事項説明), and the portal's terms say its overlay map cannot be used for that explanation. Use these maps to screen properties early, then compare with what the agent shows you and ask about any difference.
