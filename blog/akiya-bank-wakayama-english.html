Akiya bank in Wakayama: how to search cheap houses in English, subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Wakayama has one prefecture-wide entry point, the housing section of the Wakayama Life portal (わかやま暮らし), which showed 375 registered listings on 12 October 2026. It is in Japanese, so use browser translation and the keywords 空き家バンク (akiya bank), 売買 (sale) and 賃貸 (rent). The portal's FAQ says basically anyone can use it, but it does not mention nationality or visa status, so ask the municipality. Cheap houses exist: one listing on the portal on 12 October 2026 was priced at ¥1,000,000. The trade-offs are Nankai Trough tsunami exposure on the coast and landslide terrain inland, which the prefecture's own records document.

How Wakayama's akiya bank is organised

One portal, two kinds of listing. The listing search mixes properties registered with the prefectural and municipal akiya bank and properties from "housing cooperators" (住宅協力員, private real-estate firms). You can filter by sale or rent, municipality or map, price, layout, building age (up to "over 50 years"), listing date, and features such as farmland, near the sea, or VR tour available. Contact details for the municipalities and cooperators are in the directory page, which shows a count of 68 and lets you filter by whether a town has an akiya bank or subsidies.

The process. According to the usage page, you first submit a user-registration form with a pledge (paper or online). The FAQ then says you contact the municipality through the form on the property page, and that you should apply for a viewing roughly two weeks ahead. Where a broker is involved, the usual brokerage fee applies. Listed prices are generally fixed; listings marked as open to consultation can be negotiated.

The part that matters. The FAQ states that the prefecture and municipalities take no part in negotiations or contracts, accept no responsibility for disputes between the parties, and do not guarantee any property. The owner can also decline an offer. The bank is a matching service, not a purchase protection scheme. Read our akiya bank conditions guide and can foreigners buy an akiya for the general picture.

Who can use it. The FAQ says: basically anyone, but the bank mainly exists to help people looking for housing as they relocate, and business use is declined. Neither the FAQ nor the registration page mentions nationality, so a foreign buyer should confirm in writing with the specific town. The consultation line is the Wakayama Migration Support Center (わかやま移住定住支援センター), 073-422-6110; expect Japanese.

What cheap looks like: a snapshot, not a market statistic

On 12 October 2026 the first page of the portal's search showed ten listings. Sale prices ran from 100 to 1,500 (the unit is 万円 on the detail page checked, so ¥1,000,000 to ¥15,000,000), with several marked negotiable; build years ran from 1926 to 2005 where stated. That is ten listings on one day, not an average. The detail page for the cheapest sale (Shirahama, listing A-1775) shows a single-storey wooden house of about 80 years, 106.59 m² of floor area on 441.66 m² of land, a simplified water supply, a pit toilet with no sewer connection, propane gas, and a note that excavation requires an application because the site is a buried cultural property area. The price is low, and the notes show where costs and rules can hide. For realised prices, see how to check sold prices in the Ministry of Land's transaction database.

Subsidies listed on the portal

The portal's support page lists three prefectural programs as of 12 October 2026:

  • Condition survey: half of the cost of an existing-housing condition survey, up to ¥50,000, for the whole prefecture.
  • Renovation: half of the cost up to ¥1,000,000 for a home you will live in after moving from outside the prefecture, in 22 listed municipalities or areas (some areas within them excluded), with a Wakayama-based contractor involved. The page says applications had closed because the budget was reached.
  • Clean-out of belongings: up to ¥80,000 per property, for an owner who registered the property with the akiya bank and concluded a sale or rental, in the same 22 areas.

Budgets and rules change by year, so confirm with the town before counting on any of them.

Hazards specific to Wakayama

Nankai Trough tsunami

In 2026 the prefecture published revised seismic shaking and tsunami inundation assumptions for two scenarios: a frequently occurring Tokai-Tonankai-Nankai triple earthquake (Mw 8.7) and a maximum-class Nankai Trough earthquake (Mw 9.1). The maximum-class scenario builds on the Cabinet Office's March 2025 estimate with more detailed terrain data, and the page describes it as very unlikely but potentially severe. Inundation maps are published by municipality on the same site. That page does not state tsunami heights or arrival times, so read those for your town from the prefecture's maps rather than from this guide.

Landslides inland and on the Kii Peninsula

The prefecture's sediment disaster page says 21,924 sites were designated as landslide warning or special warning zones as of the end of fiscal 2025. It also lists 5,263 further sites identified from high-precision terrain data and planned for basic surveys, and warns that sites not on the list are not necessarily free of risk. A lot not yet designated can still be on a slope.

Typhoon and heavy rain

The prefecture's record of the 2011 Typhoon No. 12 disaster lists 56 dead and 5 missing, with the most casualties in Nachikatsuura (29) and Shingu (14). Rainfall exceeded 1,000 mm across a wide area over six days, 1,998 mm was recorded at Osugi in Tanabe, and 8,465 buildings were damaged. The page describes surface collapses, deep-seated collapses, debris flows and river blockages. Older houses below hillsides deserve a slope check; see the retaining wall guide.

How to check an address

Enter the address in the national Hazard Map Portal (重ねるハザードマップ, in Japanese), which overlays flood, landslide, storm surge and tsunami layers, and open the town's own map under わがまちハザードマップ. Our hazard map guide explains the layers. Compare with the prefectural maps above, since the portal notes that the agencies that made the data are the ones to ask about its interpretation.

Three things buyers miss in Wakayama

  • No sewer, and possibly no mains water. The cheapest listing on the first page had a pit toilet and a simplified water system. Budget for a septic tank (running costs) and ask about water bills and gas.
  • Heritage and land rules in the notes. A buried cultural property note means an application before digging. Also check rebuild permission (saikenchiku-fuka) and farmland status.
  • The bank does not protect you. The prefecture says it does not take part in contracts or guarantee a property, so inspection and document checks are yours to arrange.

Checklist for a Wakayama akiya

  • Register as a user on the portal, then inquire through the property page; allow about two weeks for a viewing.
  • Ask the town in writing whether foreign nationals can buy and use any subsidy.
  • Check the prefecture's current tsunami inundation map and walk the route to high ground.
  • Check the landslide warning layers and look at the slope behind and above the house.
  • Confirm water, toilet, gas and electricity arrangements, and any heritage-site notes.
  • Ask about build year and renovation needs (pre-1981 houses).
  • Arrange a building inspection (cost guide) and check the registry and boundaries before you commit.

FAQ

Where do I search for akiya in Wakayama?

The prefecture's housing portal at wakayamagurashi.jp has a listing search that covers municipal akiya bank properties and private properties from housing cooperators, a directory of municipal and cooperator contacts, and a consultation page. It showed 375 listings on 12 October 2026. It is in Japanese.

Can foreigners use the Wakayama akiya bank?

The portal's FAQ says that basically anyone can use it, though it is mainly meant to support people looking for housing as they relocate, and business use is refused. The FAQ and the registration page do not mention nationality or visa status either way, so ask the municipality in writing.

Is there a subsidy for renovating a Wakayama akiya?

The portal lists a renovation subsidy of half the cost up to ¥1,000,000 for people moving in from outside the prefecture, limited to 22 listed municipalities or areas, but the page says applications had closed because the budget was reached. It also lists a survey subsidy of half the cost up to ¥50,000 and a clean-out subsidy of up to ¥80,000 per property. Check the current status with the municipality.
