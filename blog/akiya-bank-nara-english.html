Akiya bank in Nara: how to search it in English, heritage rules and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Nara's akiya listings are scattered. We could not confirm one prefecture-wide akiya bank with an English interface; the banks are mostly run by individual cities, towns and villages in Japanese, and the national At Home / LIFULL HOME'S akiya banks add a second, municipality-sorted view. Search with browser translation and three Japanese words (空き家バンク, 売買 for sale, 賃貸 for rent), then read each municipality's own conditions. Three things set Nara apart from a typical rural prefecture: old houses can sit inside heritage or townscape districts that limit exterior changes, much of the land is steep and mountainous, and although Nara is inland it is not safe from earthquakes. Prices are not quoted here because we could not source a reliable prefecture-wide figure; read the listings and the sold-price database instead.

Where Nara's akiya listings actually are

1. Municipal banks. Nara has many city-run schemes. Nara City has an "akiya and machiya bank" (空き家・町家バンク) whose stated aims, in the city's founding rules, are to encourage moves to the eastern part of the city and to preserve the Naramachi townscape and tourism (Nara City document, December 2020; rules and support amounts may have changed since, so check the city's current page). Other municipalities, such as Sakurai and Yamatokoriyama, run their own banks. Ikoma City works with private-sector partners through a platform for vacant homes (Ikoma City press material).

2. National akiya banks. The At Home akiya bank (one of the two operators for the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism) has a page per prefecture at akiya-athome.jp; Nara's prefecture code is 29 (the page was not reachable when we checked on 3 November 2026, so we are not quoting listing counts). LIFULL HOME'S runs the other national bank. Searching both is worth the effort because small villages often list on one only.

3. Prefecture-level policy. Nara Prefecture's housing and relocation policy documents (for example its relocation support expansion paper) concentrate on the 19 municipalities of the "Oku-Yamato" mountain area. The prefecture supports information-sharing; the conditions that matter to a buyer are set by the municipality.

How these banks are structured, and what "conditions" typically mean, is covered in our akiya bank conditions for foreigners and residency requirement guides.

Searching in English when the site is Japanese

  • Search by municipality name. Copy the Japanese name (奈良市, 桜井市, 吉野町, 五條市) rather than the translated version, then add 空き家バンク.
  • Read the labels yourself. Key terms: 現況 (current state), 要リフォーム (needs renovation), 再建築不可 (cannot be rebuilt), 農地付き (farmland attached). See the kanji red-flag glossary.
  • Email in Japanese. Municipal staff rarely work in English; the copy-and-paste questions help.
  • Check the vacancy context. Housing vacancy statistics by prefecture are published by the Statistics Bureau's Housing and Land Survey on e-Stat; use it to see how common vacant houses are in your target area.

Old houses, heritage districts and townscape rules

Nara has one of Japan's densest concentrations of historic buildings, and that affects akiya in two ways. First, some districts are designated as preservation districts for groups of traditional buildings (重要伝統的建造物群保存地区), where exterior alterations normally need local approval; Agency for Cultural Affairs materials (bunka.go.jp) explain the system, and Nara has several such districts. Second, municipalities can set landscape plans under the Landscape Act that control height, colour and materials. A kominka or machiya that looks like a bargain may therefore come with limits on what you can change outside, plus a slower, more expensive renovation. None of this is automatic: ask the municipal office whether the exact lot is inside a district, and whether the building itself is a registered cultural property (登録有形文化財) or part of a landscape-protected area.

Hazards to check in Nara

Landslides and steep slopes

Much of Nara Prefecture is mountainous, and Nara designates landslide warning zones (土砂災害警戒区域) under the national Sediment Disaster Prevention Act. Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) statistics list the number of designated areas by prefecture; Nara's total is in the thousands, with most being steep-slope collapse and debris-flow areas. Check the exact lot on the national Hazard Map Portal (layers for landslide, flood and tsunami) and then the municipality's own map. Our hazard map guide shows how to read the layers, and the retaining wall guide covers the slope above or below the house. A warning zone is not an automatic reason to walk away, but it changes your questions.

Note that in 2021 the Nara Local Meteorological Observatory and the prefecture removed six lowland municipalities in the Nara Basin from the landslide-warning-information announcement area because they had no designated zones (JMA Nara notice, 28 May 2021). Basin-floor sites are therefore a different question from the mountain towns.

Earthquakes: inland does not mean safe

Nara has no coastline, so tsunami is not the issue, but strong shaking is. The Earthquake Research Committee (jishin.go.jp) evaluates the Nara Basin East Fault Zone as capable of an earthquake of about magnitude 7.4; its evaluation lists such inland faults as low-frequency but high-impact, so the 30-year probability figures are small while the damage potential is not. The Nankai Trough is the other source: it can produce strong shaking in Nara. Check the shaking and active fault layers in J-SHIS and ask for the building's age. For houses built before June 1981, read our pre-1981 seismic standard guide.

Rivers

The Yamato River basin drains the Nara Basin. Use the flood depth layers on the Hazard Map Portal, and ask which scenario (the maximum assumed rainfall or a more frequent one) the municipal map uses.

Three things buyers miss in Nara

  1. Heritage and townscape limits. The most attractive old streets are often the ones with exterior rules. Confirm district status before pricing a renovation. Our renovation cost guide explains how to build a range.
  2. Rebuilding and road access. Dense old neighbourhoods and hillside lots often have narrow lanes. A house on a road under 4 m may be unable to be rebuilt; see the rebuilding prohibition guide.
  3. Conditions that assume you stay. Banks in the Oku-Yamato area exist to attract residents. Expect questions about residence, community membership and the length of stay, and ask about repayment rules for any subsidy.

Costs: what to expect, as a range

We have not found a reliable official prefecture-wide asking-price figure, so we do not quote one. Listing prices on akiya banks range widely, from very low asking prices for remote houses to much higher ones for restored machiya near the city, and the purchase price is only part of the cost. Plan separately for the registration and acquisition costs described in our acquisition cost guide, a building inspection (home inspection), and renovation. Use the MLIT sold price database to compare real transaction prices in the same area. All of these vary by municipality and by building; treat any single number as an estimate.

Checklist before contacting a Nara akiya bank

  • Note the municipality's Japanese name and find its own akiya bank page.
  • Check the exact address on the Hazard Map Portal (landslide, flood) and in J-SHIS (shaking).
  • Ask whether the lot is inside a preservation district, landscape plan area or cultural property.
  • Ask the building's completion year and whether it meets the post-1981 seismic standard.
  • Ask about road width, rebuilding rights, and water and sewer connection.
  • Ask the municipality in writing whether foreign nationals may use the bank and its subsidies.
  • Book a building inspection before you commit.

FAQ

Is there an English version of Nara's akiya bank?

Nara has no single prefecture-wide akiya bank with an English interface that we could confirm. Banks are run by individual cities, towns and villages, mostly in Japanese, so most buyers search with browser translation and confirm details with the municipality.

Can foreigners use Nara's akiya banks?

Conditions are set by each municipality. Some banks ask buyers to live in the house, join the neighbourhood association or meet an interview step. Nationality is not necessarily mentioned, so ask the municipality in writing before applying. See can foreigners buy an akiya.

Does a heritage or townscape district stop me from renovating?

It can limit exterior changes. Districts such as preservation districts for groups of traditional buildings or municipal landscape plans may require notification or approval for alterations to the outside of a building. Ask the municipal office whether the exact lot is inside a district before you plan work.

Is Nara safe from earthquakes because it is inland?

No. Being inland removes tsunami risk but not shaking. Nara is exposed to strong shaking from the Nankai Trough and from inland active faults such as the Nara Basin East Fault Zone, so the building's age and seismic standard matter more than the prefecture's coastline.
