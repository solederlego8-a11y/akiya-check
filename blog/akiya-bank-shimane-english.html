Akiya bank in Shimane: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Shimane has no single prefectural akiya bank. Each municipality runs its own, and the Furusato Shimane Settlement Foundation's relocation portal, Kurashimanet, gathers them by region (Izumo, Iwami and Oki). Everything is in Japanese, so search with browser translation and the words 空き家バンク, 売買 and 賃貸. What sets Shimane apart for a foreign buyer is that its relocation-fund page spells out the residence statuses it accepts, instead of staying silent on nationality. Local hazards to check are Sea of Japan tsunami, landslide zones on steep valley lots and a history of shallow earthquakes.

Where Shimane's akiya listings are

The portal. The prefecture's akiya measures page says the akiya bank system is operated by municipalities and refers readers to the Furusato Shimane Settlement Foundation's site. That site, kurashimanet.jp (with offices in Matsue and the Iwami region, plus a Tokyo satellite), has a vacant-house section listing municipal banks in the Izumo area (Matsue, Izumo, Yasugi, Unnan, Iinan, Okuizumo), the Iwami area (Oda, Misato, Kawamoto, Gotsu, Onan, Hamada, Masuda, Yoshika, Tsuwano) and the Oki islands (Nishinoshima, Okinoshima). The page says to contact each municipality's department with enquiries, so the portal is a directory rather than a transaction service.

The scale. The prefecture's page cites the 2023 Housing and Land Survey: 54,600 vacant houses, 17.0 percent of 320,300 homes in Shimane. It names five dedicated akiya consultation centres (in Matsue City, Izumo City through an NPO, Iinan Town through an NPO, Kawamoto Town and Onan Town) and lists municipal contacts. It also says that akiya registered in an akiya bank qualify for an additional subsidy under its housing remodel programme (from fiscal 2020); amounts are not on that page.

National banks. At Home (akiya-athome.jp) and LIFULL HOME'S also carry Shimane municipalities that take part. Whatever the source, the listing is an introduction and the conditions are on the town's page: see our akiya bank conditions guide.

Relocation money and what it says about foreigners

The prefecture's Wakuwaku Shimane relocation support page (fiscal 2026) lists ¥1,000,000 for a household and ¥600,000 for a single person. The origin condition follows the national scheme (Cabinet Office): five of the previous ten years in Tokyo's 23 wards, or commuting to them from the Tokyo area, with a continuous year immediately before the move. You need a qualifying job (a position posted on Kurashimanet, a professional talent programme, or telework for your existing employer for at least 20 hours a week) or a related-population or start-up route, apply within a year of moving and intend to stay five years. If you move away within three years you repay in full, and half between three and five years. The scheme covers 18 municipalities, listed on the page.

The notable sentence is about status. The page requires applicants to be Japanese, or foreign nationals with the status of permanent resident, spouse of a Japanese national, spouse of a permanent resident, long-term resident or special permanent resident (永住者, 日本人の配偶者等, 永住者の配偶者等, 定住者, 特別永住者). So the page does speak to foreign residents, but it still requires Tokyo-area history, and a buyer arriving from overseas, or holding a status not on that list, would not meet it as written. Confirm with the destination town.

Hazards specific to Shimane

Sea of Japan tsunami

In March 2017 the prefecture set a tsunami inundation assumption for a maximum-class tsunami, drawing on the 2014 national study of large Sea of Japan earthquakes. The prefecture warns that flooding can occur outside the mapped area and be deeper than shown, that a larger tsunami than assumed remains possible, and that the second or third wave may have more impact than the first. It later designated tsunami disaster alert zones over that mapped area in Yasugi, Izumo, Oda, Gotsu, Hamada, Masuda, Matsue, Okinoshima, Ama, Chibu and Nishinoshima. The Matsue Local Meteorological Observatory adds that Shimane can suffer tsunami damage from Sea of Japan earthquakes even without local shaking; it lists the 1983 Sea of Japan earthquake (M7.7) and the 1993 earthquake off south-west Hokkaido (M7.8) among tsunami-producing events.

Shallow earthquakes

The same observatory page says every earthquake with its source in Shimane has been shallow. The most recent notable one, on 9 April 2018, was M6.1 at about 12 km depth, with a maximum intensity of 5-strong in Oda City and 5-weak in Izumo, Unnan, Kawamoto and Misato. For a house, that means paying attention to structure: build year, foundation and wall condition (pre-1981 houses), and what earthquake cover an insurer will offer for the address (fire insurance guide).

Landslide zones

The prefecture has designated landslide warning and special warning zones since 2005 and says it completed designation of the special warning (red) zones across the prefecture on 14 September 2021, with reviews roughly every five years. You can check a lot at the municipal office, at the prefectural civil engineering office, or on the prefecture's web map, Map on Shimane. A lot outside any zone is not guaranteed safe, so also read the hazard map guide and the retaining wall guide for hillside lots.

Three things buyers miss in Shimane

  • The portal is a directory. Conditions, prices and contacts sit with each town, so one portal visit never replaces the town's page.
  • Residence status decides the relocation fund. The page lists specific statuses; a different status is not covered by the wording.
  • A tsunami can arrive without shaking. For coastal and island lots, the map and the evacuation route matter more than local earthquake experience.

Local items to check in Shimane

  • Land title. Inheritance registration and boundary survey.
  • Farmland. A rural house may come with farmland: akiya with farmland.
  • Rebuild permission. Saikenchiku-fuka, and urbanisation control areas.
  • Septic tank. Running costs.
  • Island access. For the Oki islands, ask about the ferry, medical care and water supply.

Checklist for a Shimane akiya

  • Find the town's own akiya bank page via Kurashimanet and read the conditions.
  • Ask in writing whether foreign nationals can use the bank and which subsidies apply.
  • Check your residence status against the relocation-fund wording, if you rely on it.
  • Check the tsunami map and the landslide zones for the exact address.
  • Ask about build year, foundation and earthquake insurance availability.
  • Ask about road frontage, rebuild permission and the septic arrangement.
  • Arrange a building inspection before you commit (guide).

FAQ

Where are Shimane's akiya listings?

Each municipality runs its own akiya bank. The Furusato Shimane Settlement Foundation's Kurashimanet portal lists them by region: Izumo, Iwami and Oki. Its page tells visitors to contact each municipality directly, and the pages are in Japanese.

Can foreign nationals receive Shimane's relocation support fund?

The prefecture's page for fiscal 2026 requires applicants to be Japanese or foreign nationals holding one of these residence statuses: permanent resident, spouse of a Japanese national, spouse of a permanent resident, long-term resident or special permanent resident. The Tokyo-area residence or commuting history and the other conditions still apply.

Does Shimane have tsunami maps?

Yes. In March 2017 the prefecture set a tsunami inundation assumption for a maximum-class tsunami from Sea of Japan earthquakes and later designated tsunami disaster alert zones in coastal municipalities. The prefecture warns that flooding can occur outside the mapped area and that the second or third wave may be larger than the first.

How do I check landslide zones in Shimane?

Landslide warning zones can be confirmed at the municipal office or the prefectural civil engineering office, and on the prefecture's web map, Map on Shimane. The prefecture says designation of special warning zones was completed across the prefecture on 14 September 2021 and that zones are reviewed about every five years.
