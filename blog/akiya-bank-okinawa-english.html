Akiya bank in Okinawa: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Okinawa is the one prefecture on this list whose official relocation pages offer an English option, but the akiya itself is handled in Japanese, town by town and island by island. The prefecture's relocation site, Okinawa Shimagurashi (おきなわ島ぐらし), points to akiya banks and municipal housing subsidies; you then search the town's page with 空き家バンク, 売買 (sale) and 賃貸 (rental). The relocation fund is limited to five municipalities in fiscal 2026. The hazards that set Okinawa apart are typhoons, tsunami, and a land-purchase notification rule that applies in designated areas near important facilities and on border islands.

Where Okinawa's akiya listings are

The prefecture's relocation portal. Okinawa Shimagurashi is the prefecture's official relocation site, run by the Regional and Remote Islands Division of the Planning Department. Its housing page covers the akiya bank, a "rent first" approach (rent before you buy), property search tools and the housing subsidies that differ by municipality. The prefecture's own relocation page offers a language selection that includes English, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Korean and Spanish, and also lists a consultation service. That is a real advantage, but note that it applies to the prefecture's general pages; the pages that hold akiya conditions are generally the municipalities'.

Municipal akiya banks. On a prefecture made of one large island with small towns and many remote islands, each municipality (Nago, Uruma, Miyakojima, Ishigaki and so on) holds its own information, and the Okinawa Shimagurashi site has a page for each municipality. Many akiya banks exist to move residents in, so conditions about residence and neighbourhood participation are common; read them as described in our akiya bank conditions guide.

National banks. At Home and LIFULL HOME'S run national akiya banks that include participating Okinawa municipalities. Because Okinawa has few participating towns relative to its population, you may find that the realtor-listed used houses far outnumber the bank listings.

Searching Okinawa in English

  • Start in English, then switch to Japanese. Use the prefecture's English-language option to choose a municipality, then search 沖縄県 [town] 空き家バンク in Japanese. Add 売買 or 賃貸.
  • Rent first. The relocation site recommends renting before buying. For a house on an island you have not lived on, that is practical advice, not just a slogan.
  • Be precise about the island. 沖縄本島, 宮古島, 石垣島 and the smaller islands differ in water supply, ferry access, medical care and tsunami exposure. A listing that says 離島 (remote island) deserves extra questions about access.
  • Use the glossary. The kanji red-flag guide explains terms such as 再建築不可 and 借地. For ground that is leased see the leasehold guide.
  • Write in Japanese. The question list is ready to paste.

Relocation money in Okinawa

The prefecture's relocation support fund page lists ¥1,000,000 for a family of two or more, ¥600,000 for a single applicant and up to ¥1,000,000 more per dependant under 18. You must have lived in Tokyo's 23 wards for at least five of the ten years before moving (or commuted there from the non-disadvantaged parts of the Tokyo area), including one continuous year immediately before the move, and either work for a registered company for at least 20 hours a week on a permanent contract, take part in a specialist matching programme, or telework for your existing employer for at least 20 hours a week. Applications go to the destination municipality within a year of the move. The fund is repaid in full if you leave within three years, and by half between three and five years, with hardship exceptions.

The detail that matters most is the list of destinations. For fiscal 2026 the prefecture lists only Ishigaki City, Kunigami Village, Higashi Village, Motobu Town and Ie Village. The Cabinet Office describes the national framework in its relocation support page. Neither page mentions foreign nationals, and a buyer moving from overseas would not meet the Tokyo-origin condition as written. Housing subsidies for renovating or buying an akiya are set by municipalities, and the prefecture's relocation site says they vary; none of the pages reviewed gave a prefecture-wide figure, so ask the town.

Hazards specific to Okinawa

Typhoons

The Japan Meteorological Agency's typhoon normals for 1991 to 2020 give 7.7 approaches a year for the Okinawa region, compared with 5.8 for the Japanese mainland (and 3.8 for northern Kyushu). An "approach" means the typhoon's centre comes within 300 km of a weather station, as the JMA's Okinawa approach statistics page explains. Typhoon exposure is therefore a standing condition of ownership, and these are practical questions for any house: What are the walls and roof made of, and in what condition? Are there storm shutters or reinforced windows? How is rooftop equipment such as water tanks and solar water heaters fixed down? Is there a repair history after past typhoons? If the house is reinforced concrete, ask for an inspection for rusting reinforcement and cracked, spalling concrete, which is the sort of damage that salty sea air can accelerate; if it is timber, ask for a termite inspection report. Neither point is in an official source reviewed here, so treat them as inspection questions rather than findings, and see the home inspection guide for how a building inspection is arranged.

Tsunami

In March 2015 Okinawa Prefecture set its tsunami inundation assumption under the Tsunami Disaster Prevention and Community Building Act. It describes inundation areas and depths for a maximum-class tsunami and covers the whole coastline in two regions, the main island and the Miyako and Yaeyama islands, with detailed municipal maps in PDF. The prefecture stresses that the maximum class is based on current science and does not mean a larger tsunami cannot occur, that the maps use a 10-metre mesh that may miss small topographic features, and that the purpose is to support evacuation planning rather than to predict actual damage. For a low coastal lot, read the map sheet for the town and compare it with the national layer (hazard map guide), then work out the walking route to high ground.

Landslides and slopes

Okinawa's page on checking landslide warning zones gives three routes: the five regional civil engineering offices (Northern, Central, Southern, Miyako and Yaeyama) that keep the designation documents, the Okinawa Map Information System, and the public notices in the prefectural gazette. The prefecture warns that the online map may lag and is for rough checks only. Official confirmation is at the municipality or the civil engineering office. On a hillside lot, also read the retaining wall guide.

The land-purchase notification: Okinawa and the Important Land Survey Act

Under the Important Land Survey Act, the Cabinet Office designates special attention areas around important facilities (roughly within 1,000 metres) and on border islands. In such an area, a sale or other transfer of land of 200 square metres or more, or of a building with total floor area of 200 square metres or more, requires both seller and buyer to notify the Prime Minister before the contract. The Cabinet Office's area page points to a web map where you can search an address. We explain the rule in more detail in the Hokkaido guide. The pages reviewed do not make the duty depend on nationality. Whether a given Okinawa lot is covered depends on where it is, so ask the agent to check at the start, because it affects the timing of the contract.

Local items to check in Okinawa

  • Water and drainage on small islands. Ask how water is supplied and what the septic arrangement is (running costs).
  • Land title. Old family land can have unregistered inheritances: inheritance registration, and unsurveyed boundaries: boundary survey.
  • Leased land. If the land is not owned, see shakuchiken.
  • Shortcut to the real price. Compare asking prices with sold prices: how to check sold prices.
  • Remote purchase. If you cannot visit, read buying from overseas without visiting.

Checklist for an Okinawa akiya

  • Identify the municipality and island, then find its own akiya bank page and conditions.
  • Rent in the town first if you can.
  • Check the tsunami inundation sheet and the landslide zone for the address.
  • Ask about roof, shutters, windows, rooftop equipment and typhoon repair history.
  • Ask for a building inspection covering corrosion, cracks and termites.
  • Ask the agent to check whether the lot or building falls under the land-purchase notification.
  • Ask in writing whether foreign nationals can use the akiya bank and subsidies.

FAQ

Is there an English-language source for moving to Okinawa?

The prefecture's relocation page offers language options including English, and the official relocation site Okinawa Shimagurashi (okinawa-iju.jp) covers housing, jobs and municipal support. The listings themselves, and the municipal akiya bank rules, are in Japanese, so expect to use browser translation.

Which Okinawa municipalities are covered by the relocation support fund?

For fiscal 2026 the prefecture lists Ishigaki City, Kunigami Village, Higashi Village, Motobu Town and Ie Village. Amounts are ¥600,000 for a single person, ¥1,000,000 for a household and up to ¥1,000,000 per child, for people moving from Tokyo's 23 wards, with full repayment if you leave within three years and half between three and five years.

How often do typhoons approach Okinawa?

The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991 to 2020 normal is 7.7 approaches a year for the Okinawa region, against 5.8 for the Japanese mainland, where an approach means the centre passes within 300 km of a weather station. Check the roof, shutters, windows and any rooftop equipment for a house you are considering.

Does Okinawa publish tsunami maps?

Yes. In March 2015 the prefecture set its tsunami inundation assumption under the Tsunami Disaster Prevention and Community Building Act, covering the main island and the Miyako and Yaeyama islands as maximum-class scenarios, with municipal maps in PDF form. The prefecture notes that it does not mean a larger tsunami cannot occur.

Do I have to notify the government when I buy land in Okinawa?

Only if the property is inside a designated special attention area under the Important Land Survey Act and the land or building is 200 square metres or more; then both seller and buyer must notify the Prime Minister before the contract. The Cabinet Office's web map lets you check an address.
