Buying an akiya with farmland in Japan: the Agricultural Land Act permit (農地法3条), what changed in 2023, and how to buy the house without the field

Short answer: if any parcel in an akiya sale is farmland (農地) — a rice paddy (田) or a field (畑), including one that just looks like a garden — you cannot simply buy it with the house. Ownership of farmland passes only with the permission of the municipality's agricultural committee (農業委員会) under Article 3 of the Agricultural Land Act (農地法), and the Act states that a transfer made without that permission has no effect. Since April 2023 there is no longer a minimum farm size, which has made small plots easier to acquire, but the buyer must still show that they will actually farm all of it and work on it regularly. A buyer who lives abroad usually cannot meet that test. The common solution is to buy the house and its residential land only, and treat the field separately.

How to tell whether the sale includes farmland

Listings are not always clear, because agents describe the property as "house with land" (土地付き古家) and the field is part of the lot area. Check three places:

WhereWhat to look for
Listing農地付き (with farmland), 畑付き, 田付き, 農地法の許可が必要 (Agricultural Land Act permit required), 農地は別途 (farmland separate), 地目: 田・畑, 現況: 畑. A lot area that is suspiciously large for the price is another hint
Land registry (全部事項証明書)The land category (地目) of each parcel. 宅地 = residential; 田 = paddy; 畑 = field; 山林 = forest; 原野 = wasteland; 雑種地 = miscellaneous. A sale often covers several parcels with different categories — ask for every parcel number (地番)
Farmland mapThe Ministry of Agriculture's nationwide farmland map, eMAFF農地ナビ (Farmland Navi), shows parcels the agricultural committee treats as farmland, and whether they are inside an agricultural promotion zone

One important rule: whether land is farmland under the Act is decided by its actual condition (現況主義), not by the registry. A parcel registered as 宅地 but cultivated as a field can be farmland; a parcel registered as 畑 that has turned into forest may be certified as no longer farmland (非農地証明) by the committee. Never assume from the registry alone — ask the agent which parcels need a permit.

What the agricultural committee checks (Article 3 permit)

An Article 3 permit is needed to acquire ownership of farmland, or to rent it, when it will stay farmland. The committee — one per municipality — examines the application against the requirements in Article 3(2) of the Agricultural Land Act:

RequirementJapaneseWhat it means in practice
All land used efficiently全部効率利用要件You must farm all the farmland you will own or rent, not just part of it, with the machinery, labour and skills to do so. Committees typically ask for a farming plan: crops, tools, who does the work
Regular personal involvement農作業常時従事要件For an individual, the buyer (or household members) must be regularly engaged in the farm work. Committees commonly use a guideline of around 150 days a year; practice varies by municipality
Harmony with the area地域との調和要件Your use must not disrupt the surrounding farmers — for example by cutting off shared irrigation, planting crops that spread pests, or refusing to join water management
Minimum farm size下限面積要件Abolished from 1 April 2023. Previously a buyer had to farm at least 50 ares (5,000 m²) in total, reduced locally in many areas; that requirement no longer exists

Since 1 September 2023, applications to acquire ownership must also state the applicant's nationality (国籍等) — this is a reporting item, not a nationality ban. Foreign nationals can obtain Article 3 permission on the same tests as anyone else. The difficulty for most foreign buyers is not nationality but the regular-involvement requirement: if you will live abroad, or only visit for a few weeks a year, the committee is unlikely to find that you will farm the land yourself. See the abolition notice from a municipality such as Funabashi City (Japanese) for how committees describe the post-2023 rules.

Committees usually meet once a month and have a monthly application deadline, so a permit takes from several weeks to a couple of months. The sales contract should make the farmland part conditional on the permit (農地法の許可を停止条件とする), so that you are not obliged to pay for land you cannot legally receive.

Converting farmland to a garden, parking or a new house

If you want to use the field for anything other than farming — a parking space, an extension, a new house, even a lawn — that is conversion (転用) and needs a different permit:

  • Article 4 covers an owner converting their own farmland; Article 5 covers acquiring farmland in order to convert it. Both need permission from the prefectural governor (or a designated municipality), with the agricultural committee as the window.
  • Inside an urbanization promotion area (市街化区域), conversion only needs a prior notification (届出) to the committee — a much lighter process.
  • Outside it, permission depends on the class of farmland. Land inside an agricultural promotion zone (農用地区域, often called 青地 "green land") is in principle not convertible unless it is first removed from the zone (農振除外), which is slow and often refused. High-quality first-class farmland (第1種農地) is in principle not convertible either. Land near stations and built-up areas (第3種農地) is generally convertible.
  • If the house is also in an urbanization control area (市街化調整区域), the City Planning Act adds its own restrictions; see urbanization control areas.

Do not count on converting a field to a building plot when you price the property. Ask the agricultural committee before you sign: この農地は農用地区域内ですか。転用の見込みはありますか (Is this farmland inside the agricultural promotion zone? Is conversion realistic?).

What happens if you ignore the permit

  • The transfer of the farmland is not effective, so the registry cannot be changed into your name. You will have paid for land you do not own.
  • Converting farmland without permission can lead to orders to restore it to farmland, and criminal penalties under the Act.
  • Some sellers suggest a "provisional registration" (仮登記) to hold the field until a permit is obtained some day. This leaves the land in the seller's name — and in their heirs' names later — and is a common source of disputes. Treat it with great caution and ask a judicial scrivener before agreeing.

Practical points that affect the price and running costs

  • Water rights and irrigation fees: paddy fields belong to a land improvement district (土地改良区) that charges annual fees (賦課金) and expects participation in channel cleaning. Ask: 土地改良区の賦課金はいくらですか。
  • Neighbour expectations: an uncultivated field next to working farmland spreads weeds and pests. Even if you only buy the house, the state of the seller's adjoining field will affect your relationship with the neighbours.
  • Fixed asset tax: farmland is taxed lightly while it remains farmland; the house parcel is taxed as residential land. See fixed asset tax and acquisition costs.
  • Boundaries: a house sold separately from the field needs a clear line between the two, which can mean a survey and a subdivision registration (分筆). See land boundary surveys.

FAQ

Can a foreigner buy farmland in Japan?

Nationality is not a bar. Since September 2023 applicants must state their nationality, but the permit is decided on the same tests for everyone: farming all the land efficiently, regular personal involvement in the work, and harmony with the area. Living abroad usually makes the second test impossible to meet.

Is there still a minimum area for buying farmland?

No. The minimum area requirement (下限面積) in Article 3 was abolished from 1 April 2023. Small plots attached to an akiya are therefore no longer automatically excluded, but the other requirements still apply.

The registry says 宅地 but there is a vegetable patch. Is that farmland?

Possibly. Farmland status is judged by actual use. A small kitchen garden inside a residential lot is normally treated as part of the house plot, but a large cultivated area can be treated as farmland. Ask the agent and, if in doubt, the agricultural committee.

Do I need a farming visa to buy a farmhouse?

Buying a farmhouse or farmland does not give or require a visa. The house itself can be bought by anyone; only the farmland part depends on the agricultural committee's permit.

How long does the agricultural committee permit take?

Committees generally meet monthly, so a straightforward Article 3 application takes several weeks to two months from submission. Conversion permits for land outside urbanization promotion areas take longer.
