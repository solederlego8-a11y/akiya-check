Akiya bank in Yamagata: cheap houses, English search tips, snow and landslide checks (2026)

Short answer: Yamagata has no English-language akiya portal that could be confirmed. Akiya banks in the prefecture are run by individual municipalities (in Tsuruoka City, by a non-profit), the prefecture points searchers to a relocation-portal list plus two national banks (LIFULL HOME'S and At Home), and the eight Mogami-region municipalities share one joint portal. Searching "cheap houses in Yamagata" therefore means browser translation and reading each town's own rules. The pages checked for this guide did not state nationality conditions, but conditions differ by bank, so ask in writing. Before a low price excites you, check three things for the exact address: winter snow cost (the whole prefecture is a designated heavy snow area), landslide zoning, and rebuilding rights. Prices and subsidies change, so confirm with the municipality.

Where Yamagata's akiya listings are

1. Municipal banks, found through the prefecture. The prefecture's housing information centre (Tatekkana) says that in municipalities with an akiya bank, vacant-house listings can be viewed online, and that some banks let you find inexpensive properties "not circulating on the market" (prefectural page; the page shows no update date). It links to the relocation portal's akiya bank list, which could not be loaded when checked for this guide, so the number of municipalities on it is unconfirmed; we cannot say how many municipalities appear on it. It does not state eligibility rules or fees; those sit with each bank.

2. A regional portal in Mogami. The prefecture announced on 29 January 2025 that the akiya banks of the eight municipalities in the Mogami region were combined into one site, Mogami Akiya no Mori, opening 1 February 2025 (prefectural announcement). The announcement did not name the eight municipalities.

3. National banks. The prefectural page lists LIFULL HOME'S and At Home for nationwide listings. Use them to see which towns have stock, then go to the town's page. Our guide to akiya bank conditions covers the typical registration flow, and the Akita and Niigata guides apply the same approach to neighbouring snow-country prefectures. Search the Japanese term 山形県 [town name] 空き家バンク.

What the official pages say

An operator example: Tsuruoka City

Tsuruoka City's akiya bank page says the program is operated by the non-profit Tsuruoka Land Bank (つるおかランド・バンク), set up jointly by private businesses and the city. Owners who want to sell or lease register a house and the bank introduces it to prospective users; buyer registration steps are left to the operator's own site. The page does not state nationality or settlement conditions or fees, and it is dated 9 August 2019, so treat it as a pointer rather than current rules. This is one municipality; others may work differently.

Support and subsidies

The prefecture's support page lists a national tax deduction of up to 30 million yen on capital gains when certain earthquake-resistant vacant houses (or the site after demolition) are sold, renovation subsidies run by municipalities whose requirements and amounts vary, and a prefectural used-home purchase subsidy. It gives no amounts for the last two and no date. It also lists a free akiya general information desk (023-679-5255, weekday daytime hours) and 19 local consultation desks that cover sales, rentals, inheritance and demolition. Treat any subsidy as unconfirmed until the municipality confirms it in writing; many carry residency or contractor conditions.

Landslide and flood: use the official maps

Yamagata Prefecture's landslide zone page reports 5,243 landslide warning zones and 3,546 special warning zones as of 6 March 2026, with a municipality-by-municipality list. The prefecture is also extracting new candidate areas for survey, so a lot outside a designated zone today is not proof of safety. For the exact address, overlay flood, landslide and tsunami data on the Hazard Map Portal (MLIT and the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan); our hazard map walkthrough explains the colours. Mountain-edge and slope lots also raise retaining-wall questions (retaining wall guide). We did not find a prefecture-wide flood figure in official sources, so check flood depth per address.

Snow: the cost nobody lists

According to the prefecture's Fourth Basic Plan for Snow Countermeasures (March 2020), the whole prefecture is designated a heavy snow area under the Special Measures Act, and 26 municipalities are designated special heavy snow areas. For merged municipalities such as Tsuruoka, Sakata and Shonai, only parts of the town carry the special designation, so check the exact address. A summer listing photo shows nothing about roof snow load, who clears the roof, or whether the access road is ploughed. Our guide to buying in Japan's heavy snow areas lists the questions to send. Also ask about heating fuel (propane vs city gas) and whether a well or septic tank could freeze (well water, jokaso guide).

Three things buyers miss in Yamagata

  1. Rebuilding rights and zoning. A cheap rural house may sit on land where rebuilding is restricted or in an urbanisation control area; see rebuilding-prohibited properties and urbanisation control areas. Houses attached to farmland can also need a permit (farmland guide).
  2. Landslide-zone lots. With thousands of designated zones prefecture-wide, a valley or hillside house may fall inside one. Ask for the municipal zone drawing and read what the special warning zone restricts before relying on a renovation plan.
  3. Registry and inheritance gaps. Many akiya have not had ownership updated after a death. Read the registry before paying a deposit (registry guide, inheritance registration).

Costs and prices

No reliable Yamagata-wide average price was found in official sources for this guide, so none is given here. The prefecture only says some banks list inexpensive properties that are not otherwise on the market. To see what comparable houses actually sold for, use MLIT's Real Estate Information Library, explained in our sold-price guide. Checklist before you contact a Yamagata akiya bank

  • Note the municipality's Japanese name and find its bank from the prefectural list (or the Mogami portal).
  • Ask who operates the bank and whether foreign residents may register.
  • Check the exact address on the Hazard Map Portal and ask for the municipal landslide zone drawing.
  • Ask whether the address is in a special heavy snow area, who clears roof and road, and what winter running costs were.
  • Ask about water source, septic tank records and heating fuel.
  • Confirm road access and rebuilding rights in the registry and zoning.
  • Ask whether any renovation or purchase subsidy is open, and what residency strings it has.
  • Book an inspection before paying a deposit.

FAQ

Is there an English version of Yamagata's akiya bank?

Not that could be confirmed. Yamagata's akiya banks are run by individual municipalities and the pages checked for this guide are in Japanese. Browser translation works for reading; confirm conditions with the municipality in writing.

Can a foreigner use a Yamagata akiya bank?

The prefectural and Tsuruoka City pages checked for this guide do not state nationality conditions. Eligibility is set by each bank, so ask the operator directly and do not assume either way.

How cheap are houses in Yamagata?

No reliable prefecture-wide price figure was found in official sources for this guide. The prefecture says some akiya banks list inexpensive properties not found on the open market. Check comparable sold prices in MLIT's Real Estate Information Library and budget separately for repairs, snow removal and taxes.
