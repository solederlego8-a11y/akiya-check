Akiya bank in Nagasaki: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Nagasaki's akiya banks are run by 17 municipalities and gathered on the prefecture's relocation site, Nagasaki Iju Navi (ながさき移住ナビ). The pages are in Japanese, so search with browser translation and the words 空き家バンク, 売買 and 賃貸. The prefecture's own contract figures show that outsiders do buy and rent through these banks, and that the island of Goto and the island of Iki lead. The relocation fund follows the Tokyo-origin rules. The hazard records buyers should read are the 1982 Nagasaki heavy rain, in which most deaths came from landslides and debris flows, and the Unzen Fugendake eruption of 1990 to 1995.

Where Nagasaki's akiya listings are

The prefecture's list. The akiya bank list page says 17 municipalities operate akiya banks and that, at the end of fiscal 2024, 384 properties were registered as available, with the number changing daily. It offers a map and links grouped by region, and notes that each municipality runs its own bank, that some municipalities have none and that you contact the municipality's department directly. The site is the prefecture's official relocation support site. The housing section also covers housing subsidies and a support centre for house-hunting questions, in Japanese.

What the figures show. The prefecture's fiscal 2023 akiya bank summary reports 325 properties registered at the end of the year, 330 new registrations in fiscal 2023 (320 the year before) and 197 completed contracts: 100 rentals (51 to people from within the prefecture, 49 from outside) and 97 sales (62 within, 35 from outside). That makes 84 contracts with people from outside the prefecture. The municipalities with the most contracts were Goto (55), Iki (22), Minamishimabara (21), Unzen (18) and Shinkamigoto (12). Two lessons: the volume is modest, and it is concentrated in a few places, so ask how many listings your target town actually has.

National banks. At Home (akiya-athome.jp) and LIFULL HOME'S also carry participating Nagasaki towns. Whatever the source, the conditions are on the municipality's page: see our akiya bank conditions guide.

Read the road frontage (接道), land category and the heating and water arrangements on every listing, and use the kanji red-flag guide for the rest. Nagasaki is hilly, so slope and access matter: see the retaining wall guide. The agent question list is Japanese ready to paste.

Relocation money

The prefecture's relocation support page (fiscal 2026) lists ¥1,000,000 for a household and ¥600,000 for a single person, plus up to ¥1,000,000 per child under 18, and a start-up support of up to ¥2,000,000. The origin condition follows the national scheme (Cabinet Office): five or more of the previous ten years in the Tokyo 23 wards, or living in the non-disadvantaged Tokyo area and commuting to them. You also need a qualifying job (the prefecture's N-Navi Career site), a professional-talent route, a funded start-up, remote work or related-population status set by the municipality; you apply within a year of moving and intend to stay five years. The page lists 20 municipalities, and full repayment applies for a false application, a move within three years or leaving the job within one year, with half repayment for a move between three and five years. The page did not mention nationality, and a buyer arriving from overseas would not meet the Tokyo-origin condition as written. Municipalities add their own conditions, so confirm with the destination town.

Hazards specific to Nagasaki

The 1982 Nagasaki heavy rain

The Cabinet Office's case record of the July 1982 heavy rain describes record-breaking short-duration rainfall that left 299 people dead or missing, and says that about 80 percent of the deaths came from landslides and debris flows rather than direct flooding. Its separate lessons report is the fuller account. For a hillside lot in a town or on an island, the question is not only whether the house is sound but what is above it: check the landslide layer on the national hazard map and the municipality's own map, and ask about drainage and the condition of any retaining wall.

The Unzen Fugendake eruption, 1990 to 1995

The Cabinet Office's record of the Unzen Fugendake eruption describes a large pyroclastic flow on 3 June 1991 that caused many casualties, and a warning zone that was expanded until, by September, 2,047 households and 7,208 residents were evacuated. Its lessons report covers the 1990 to 1995 activity. This matters for akiya on the Shimabara Peninsula, where Unzen and Minamishimabara were among the municipalities with the most akiya bank contracts in fiscal 2023: read the volcano and debris-flow information for the town and ask what the municipality says about evacuation.

Typhoons and islands

The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991 to 2020 normals count 3.8 typhoon approaches a year for northern Kyushu, against 5.8 for the Japanese mainland as a whole (JMA). For island houses, ask about roof and window condition and about ferry, air and medical access (inspection guide).

Three things buyers miss in Nagasaki

  • The listings are few and concentrated. Goto alone had 55 of the 197 contracts in fiscal 2023.
  • The slope above the house is part of the house. The 1982 record points to landslides and debris flows, not only river flooding.
  • The sea is a logistics risk. On islands, access, medical care and supplies shape daily life as much as the building does.

Local items to check in Nagasaki

  • Land title. Inheritance registration and boundary survey.
  • Rebuild permission. Steep, narrow lots often hit the road-frontage rule: saikenchiku-fuka.
  • Leased land. Shakuchiken.
  • Septic tank. Running costs.
  • Real price. How to check sold prices.

Checklist for a Nagasaki akiya

  • Find the town's own akiya bank page from the prefecture's list and read the conditions.
  • Ask how many listings the town has and whether foreign nationals can use the bank and any subsidy.
  • Check the landslide and debris-flow layers for the address, and for the lot above it.
  • Near Unzen, read the volcano information the municipality publishes.
  • Ask about road frontage, rebuild permission and the septic arrangement.
  • For islands, ask about ferry or air access, water and medical care.
  • Arrange a building inspection before you commit.

FAQ

How many municipalities in Nagasaki run an akiya bank?

The prefecture's relocation site says 17 municipalities operate akiya banks, with 384 properties registered as available at the end of fiscal 2024, a number that changes daily. Each municipality runs its own bank, and the site links to them by region. The pages are in Japanese.

Are Nagasaki's akiya sold to people from outside the prefecture?

Yes. For fiscal 2023 the prefecture's site reports 197 contracts: 100 rentals, 49 of them to people from outside the prefecture, and 97 sales, 35 of them to people from outside, so 84 contracts in all involved people from outside the prefecture. The leading municipalities by contracts were Goto (55), Iki (22), Minamishimabara (21), Unzen (18) and Shinkamigoto (12).

Does Nagasaki have a relocation support fund?

For fiscal 2026 the prefecture lists ¥1,000,000 for a household and ¥600,000 for a single person for people who lived in the Tokyo 23 wards, or commuted to them from the non-disadvantaged Tokyo area, for five of the previous ten years, covering 20 listed municipalities. Full repayment applies if you move away within three years and half between three and five years. The pages reviewed did not mention nationality.

What are Nagasaki's major disaster records?

The Cabinet Office's case records describe the July 1982 Nagasaki heavy rain, which left 299 people dead or missing, and the 1990 to 1995 Unzen Fugendake eruption, whose large pyroclastic flow of 3 June 1991 caused many casualties and led to an expanded warning zone. Both are reasons to check landslide, debris-flow and volcano maps for a specific address.
