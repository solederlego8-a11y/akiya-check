Akiya bank in Kumamoto: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and earthquake checks (2026)

Short answer: Kumamoto Prefecture runs one search site for most of its towns, the Kumamoto Prefecture Akiya Bank Platform, but four large cities (Kumamoto, Arao, Aso and Amakusa) keep their own banks that the platform does not show. It is in Japanese, so search with browser translation and the words 空き家バンク, 売買 and 賃貸. The point to put first is the ground: Kumamoto had two intensity-7 earthquakes in April 2016, and on 28 July 2026 the Japan Meteorological Agency recorded a magnitude 7.1 earthquake with intensity 7 in Uki City and Hikawa Town. If a listing is in the affected area, damage and repair history come before price.

Where Kumamoto's akiya listings are

The prefecture's platform. The prefecture's announcement says the Akiya Bank Platform began operating on 1 April 2023 with 33 participating municipalities, pulling municipal listings into one wide-area search with pictogram-based search and 360-degree virtual viewing. The site lets you search sale (売買) and rental (賃貸) listings by conditions, on a map or by area, and offers a house-search manual and a page on each municipality's support schemes. Its notice says Kumamoto City, Arao City, Aso City and Amakusa City operate separate banks that cannot be viewed on the platform, so for those cities go to the city's own page. The platform is run by the prefecture's Regional Revitalisation Division (096-333-2155).

Relocation portal and consultation. The prefecture lists relocation consultation centres in Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka and Kumamoto on its relocation support centre page. Expect Japanese.

National banks. At Home (akiya-athome.jp) and LIFULL HOME'S also carry participating Kumamoto towns. A listing is an introduction; the conditions on residence, renovation and neighbourhood participation are on the town's page, as described in our akiya bank conditions guide.

Relocation money

The prefecture's fiscal 2026 relocation support page says the prefecture and its municipalities, "excluding some", pay the fund to people moving from the Tokyo area: ¥1,000,000 for a household of two or more and ¥600,000 for a single person, with extra amounts per child under 18 for moves to specified municipalities. The origin condition follows the national scheme (Cabinet Office): five of the previous ten years in Tokyo's 23 wards or commuting to them, with the last year continuous. You need a qualifying job on the matching site, prefecture-backed start-up funding, remote work (20 hours a week or more) or related-population status set by the municipality, and you apply within a year of moving and intend to stay five years. The page says the payment is taxable income. Kumamoto City does not appear in the list of covered municipalities. The pages reviewed did not mention nationality, and a buyer arriving from overseas would not meet the Tokyo-origin condition as written.

Earthquakes: 2016 and 2026

The 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes

The Fire and Disaster Management Agency's summary records a magnitude 6.5 earthquake at 21:26 on 14 April 2016 with intensity 7 in Mashiki Town, and a magnitude 7.3 earthquake at 01:25 on 16 April, 28 hours later, with intensity 7 in Mashiki and Nishihara; it calls this the first time in Japan's seismic observation history that two intensity-7 earthquakes occurred in succession. The prefecture's tenth-anniversary page counts 275 deaths (50 direct and 225 related), 2,739 injured and 198,385 damaged houses: 8,657 completely destroyed, 34,489 half destroyed and 155,239 partially damaged.

The earthquake of 28 July 2026

The Japan Meteorological Agency's press release records a preliminary magnitude 7.1 earthquake at about 16:27 on 28 July 2026, with intensity 7 in Uki City and Hikawa Town, of the strike-slip fault type, and a tsunami advisory for the Ariake and Yatsushiro seas. Its earthquake portal lists aftershocks, including a magnitude 6.1 event near Amakusa and Ashikita, and says elevated earthquake activity will continue for some time. Because the ground may have become fragile, the agency also applied provisional lower thresholds for landslide warnings, at 70 percent or 80 percent of the normal criteria, for municipalities in Kumamoto and, at 80 percent, in Nagasaki and Kagoshima; check whether those are still in force. The prefecture's page on emergency rental housing for the "Reiwa 8 Kumamoto earthquake" (updated 31 July 2026) covers 20 municipalities. The Cabinet Office publishes damage reports on its earthquake page; the figures are provisional and change, so we do not repeat them here.

What a buyer should ask

For any listing in a municipality affected in 2016 or 2026, ask in writing: Was the house inspected or assessed after the earthquakes, and what was the result? What was repaired, when, and by whom? Are there records of the repair? Has the foundation or the ground around the house shifted, and does the lot sit on a slope or a retaining wall (retaining wall guide)? What cover will an insurer give for the address (fire insurance guide)? Then commission your own building inspection (inspection guide) and check build year and foundation (pre-1981 houses). These are inspection questions, not findings: no official source we reviewed rates individual houses.

Other hazards

Check the flood and landslide layers on the national hazard map for river and hillside lots. JMA's 1991 to 2020 normals count 3.8 typhoon approaches a year for northern Kyushu and 4.3 for the Amami and southern Kyushu region, against 5.8 for the whole mainland (JMA).

Three things buyers miss in Kumamoto

  • The platform is not the whole prefecture. Four large cities are not on it.
  • An earthquake leaves records. After 2016 and 2026, a house's damage assessment and repair history are part of its price.
  • Warnings can change. Provisional landslide thresholds were lowered after the July 2026 earthquake; check the current status.

Local items to check in Kumamoto

  • Land title. Inheritance registration and boundary survey.
  • Farmland. Akiya with farmland.
  • Rebuild permission. Saikenchiku-fuka and urbanisation control areas.
  • Reclaimed or soft ground. Liquefaction check.

Checklist for a Kumamoto akiya

  • Search the platform, then open the town's own page for conditions; for the four large cities use their own sites.
  • Ask in writing whether foreign nationals can use the bank and any subsidy.
  • If the town was affected in 2016 or 2026, ask for the assessment and repair records.
  • Check the flood and landslide layers for the address, and the current status of landslide warnings.
  • Check build year, foundation and the ground around the house.
  • Ask what earthquake cover an insurer will provide.
  • Arrange a building inspection before you commit.

FAQ

Is there a prefecture-wide akiya bank for Kumamoto?

Yes. The Kumamoto Prefecture Akiya Bank Platform (kumamoto-akiya360.jp) began operating on 1 April 2023 with 33 participating municipalities and lets you search sale and rental listings by conditions, map or area. Kumamoto City, Arao City, Aso City and Amakusa City run separate banks that cannot be viewed on the platform. It is in Japanese.

Was there a new earthquake in Kumamoto in 2026?

Yes. The Japan Meteorological Agency recorded a magnitude 7.1 earthquake in Kumamoto at about 16:27 on 28 July 2026, with a maximum intensity of 7 in Uki City and Hikawa Town. The agency's portal says elevated earthquake activity will continue for some time. Damage figures are provisional and published by the Cabinet Office, so check the latest report for the town you are considering.

How bad was the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake?

The prefecture's tenth-anniversary page counts 275 deaths, of which 50 were direct earthquake deaths and 225 related deaths, and 198,385 damaged houses: 8,657 completely destroyed, 34,489 half destroyed and 155,239 partially damaged. Two earthquakes of intensity 7 struck 28 hours apart, on 14 and 16 April 2016.

Does Kumamoto's relocation fund cover Kumamoto City?

The prefecture's fiscal 2026 page says the scheme applies in the prefecture and its municipalities except some, and Kumamoto City does not appear in the list of covered municipalities. It lists ¥1,000,000 for a household and ¥600,000 for a single person for people from the Tokyo area, and the pages reviewed did not mention nationality.
