Can foreigners get a loan to buy a house in Japan? What buyers do without a mortgage

Short answer

Most foreign buyers who do not live and earn in Japan cannot rely on a Japanese mortgage. Plan on cash purchase, a loan in your home country, or a smaller property — and confirm eligibility before you fall in love with a listing.

Why a Japanese mortgage is hard for non-resident buyers

Japanese housing loans are built around borrowers who live in Japan, earn income in Japan, and can be credit-checked through Japanese systems. Many lenders ask for permanent residency or a long-term work visa, Japanese tax records, and employment with a Japanese employer. If you live overseas, work for a foreign employer, and have no credit history in Japan, you may not pass the first screening — even if the property itself is inexpensive.

This is not a rule that foreigners can never borrow. A foreign national who lives and works in Japan, pays tax in Japan, and holds a stable status of residence is in a very different position from an overseas buyer. The practical question is not your nationality alone, but where you live, where your income comes from, and whether a lender can verify both.

Questions to ask a lender before you search seriously

  • Do you lend to applicants living outside Japan?
  • Which status of residence and how many years of Japanese tax records do you require?
  • Can foreign-source income be counted, and in which currency?
  • Is a Japanese guarantor, spouse, or local bank account required?
  • What loan-to-value and minimum loan amount apply to a low-priced rural property?

Ask in writing and keep the answers. Low-priced akiya create a second problem: even eligible borrowers may find that the loan amount is too small, the building is too old, or the land type falls outside a lender's criteria. Eligibility for a loan in general is not the same as eligibility for that specific house.

What buyers do without a mortgage

1. Buy with cash and keep the budget honest

Cash is the most common route for overseas akiya buyers. It removes lender screening, but it does not remove the other costs. As explained in our guide to the real cost of buying an akiya, closing fees, taxes, and renovation can exceed the listing price. A cash budget should include all three buckets plus a reserve, not just the price.

2. Borrow in your home country

Some buyers use a home-country loan, a secured credit line, or savings products in their own banking system. That decision belongs with your own lender and tax adviser: currency risk, repayment in another currency, and the tax treatment in both countries need to be understood before you commit.

3. Choose a smaller or cheaper property

If the total cash needed is the constraint, a smaller house, a different municipality, or a property needing less immediate renovation may fit better than stretching for a larger one. Compare total cost, not listing price, across candidates.

4. Buy jointly or through a structure — with advice

Joint purchase with a resident spouse or partner, or buying through a company, changes the loan and tax picture. These routes have legal and tax consequences and should only be used after advice from a judicial scrivener and a tax professional who understand your situation.

A realistic order of steps

  1. Decide your total cash ceiling, including fees and renovation reserve
  2. If you hope to borrow in Japan, ask lenders about eligibility before paying for travel
  3. Screen listings against the total-cost ceiling, not the asking price
  4. Check zoning, hazard and transaction data for the shortlist before visiting
  5. Confirm the payment route and schedule with the agent and scrivener before signing

Summary

Do not build a purchase plan on a Japanese mortgage until a lender has confirmed, in writing, that someone in your situation can borrow for that type of property. Most overseas buyers plan for cash, prepare the full-cost budget, and treat any loan as a bonus rather than the foundation.

FAQ

Q. Can a foreigner get a housing loan in Japan?

Some can, especially those who live and work in Japan with a stable status of residence and Japanese income records. Non-resident buyers with foreign income often do not meet lender criteria. Only a lender can confirm your case, so ask before you rely on a loan.

Q. Does paying cash make buying an akiya simpler?

It removes lender screening and can make scheduling easier, but registration, taxes, contracts and renovation still apply. You still need an agent, a judicial scrivener for registration, and a full-cost budget.

Q. Should I transfer the full purchase money to Japan early?

Do not move large sums before the contract terms, payment schedule and receiving account are confirmed in writing through the agent and professionals handling the purchase. Follow their instructions and your own bank's checks.
