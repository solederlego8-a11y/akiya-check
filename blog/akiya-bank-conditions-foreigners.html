Akiya Bank Conditions for Foreigners: Residency Rules, Renovation Deadlines, Age Limits and How to Actually Apply (2026)

Direct answer: An akiya bank (空き家バンク) is a listing board run by a municipality, not a seller. Foreigners can usually use one, but most listings carry conditions the English-language "free houses in Japan" articles leave out: you must live in the house (often as a registered resident), renovate it by a deadline, sometimes be under a certain age, and often join the neighbourhood association. The house is cheap because the town is buying a resident, not selling a property. This guide explains how the system works, the conditions to expect, and what to ask the town office before you apply.

In three lines

  • The municipality introduces; the private owner sells. Conditions come from the town, the price comes from the owner.
  • Expect some mix of: residency (定住), renovation deadline, age limit, neighbourhood participation, and a ban on resale or short-term rental for several years.
  • Nationality is rarely a condition. Residence status often is — you cannot "register as a resident" on a tourist visa.

What an akiya bank actually is

Since the 2010s most Japanese municipalities have run an akiya bank: owners of empty houses register them, the town publishes the listing, and interested buyers or renters apply through the town. There are two national aggregators, both commissioned by the Ministry of Land (MLIT): the 全国版空き家・空き地バンク operated by LIFULL HOME'S and the one operated by at home. They pull listings from several hundred municipalities but not all 1,741, so the town's own page is still the primary source.

Three things follow from the structure. First, the town does not own the house and cannot negotiate the price for you. Second, the town's staff are administrators, not agents — many banks require you to use a local real-estate agent for the contract, and some towns have a partner agent list. Third, the town's conditions exist because the bank's purpose is population policy: the programme is judged on how many people move in, not on how many houses are sold.

The conditions to expect, in the wording you will see

1. 定住 / 居住要件 — residency

The most common condition. Typical wording: 「購入後◯年以内に住民登録し、◯年以上定住すること」 — register as a resident within N years and stay for N years. This is the condition that filters foreign buyers most, not because of nationality but because 住民登録 (resident registration) requires a residence status that allows it. A tourist or a 90-day visa-free stay does not. Long-term residents, spouses, permanent residents and business-manager visa holders can register.

2. 改修期限 — renovation deadline

「◯年以内に改修し居住可能な状態にすること」 — renovate to a livable standard within N years. Often paired with a renovation subsidy (改修補助金), which is where a "¥0 house" turns into a real budget: subsidies typically cover a share of the cost up to a cap, and the rest is yours.

3. 年齢要件 — age limits

Some programmes, especially the ones that combine a house with a moving subsidy (移住支援金), are limited to applicants under a certain age or to households with children. This is stated on the programme page, not the listing.

4. 地域活動への参加 — neighbourhood participation

「自治会に加入し地域活動に参加すること」 — join the neighbourhood association and take part in its activities (cleaning days, festivals, fees). In small villages this is a real expectation, and English-speaking applicants are sometimes interviewed about it.

5. 転売・民泊の禁止 — no resale, no short-term rental

Many banks prohibit reselling within N years and using the house as a minpaku (short-term rental). If your plan is a holiday let, ask before you apply.

6. 審査 — screening

Some towns interview applicants or require a written plan (利用計画書). This is normal and not a sign of discrimination; it applies to everyone.

Can foreigners apply?

In most municipalities, yes. Japan has no nationality restriction on owning land or buildings (full guide), and akiya banks generally inherit that. What excludes foreign applicants in practice is the residency condition described above, and — less formally — the fact that the application, interview and contract are in Japanese. Bring a Japanese-speaking friend, a bilingual agent, or a translator to the town office meeting; the staff will rarely have English.

What "¥0" and "¥100,000" really cost

  • Acquisition costs: registration tax, acquisition tax, judicial scrivener fee, and — if a partner agent handles the contract — an agent fee. Budget ¥200,000–400,000 even on a token-price house.
  • Fixed asset tax every year, based on the assessed value, not the price you paid.
  • Renovation: the condition, not the option. Pre-1981 houses (旧耐震) may also need seismic work.
  • Utilities: rural akiya frequently have a septic tank (浄化槽, roughly ¥50,000–80,000 a year to maintain) and propane instead of city gas. See the 10 red flags glossary.

The questions to ask the town office

Paste these into an email to the akiya bank contact (usually 空き家バンク担当 in the 移住定住 or 都市計画 section):

  • 「外国籍でも申請できますか？在留資格の要件はありますか？」 — Can foreign nationals apply? Is there a residence-status requirement?
  • 「居住要件（住民登録の期限、定住年数）を教えてください。」 — What are the residency conditions (registration deadline, years)?
  • 「改修の期限と、改修補助金の上限・補助率を教えてください。」 — Renovation deadline and subsidy cap/rate?
  • 「年齢や世帯構成の要件はありますか？」 — Any age or household requirements?
  • 「転売・賃貸・民泊の制限はありますか？」 — Restrictions on resale, renting, short-term rental?
  • 「契約は所有者と直接ですか、仲介業者を通しますか？仲介手数料はかかりますか？」 — Contract direct or through an agent? Agent fee?
  • 「条件を満たせなかった場合のペナルティ（補助金の返還等）を教えてください。」 — What is the penalty for not meeting a condition?

Before you apply: check the lot, not just the programme

Akiya bank listings go through the same checks as any other listing: road frontage and rebuild permission, hazard zones, septic and propane, disclosure matters. Towns list houses as they are; a listing on a municipal site is not a warranty. FAQ

Can foreigners use a Japanese akiya bank?

Usually yes. Most have no nationality rule; the conditions are about use (living there, renovating, joining the neighbourhood). Programmes that require resident registration need a residence status that allows it.

Are akiya bank houses really free?

Some are listed at ¥0 or a token price, but acquisition costs, yearly tax and the required renovation are real. A ¥0 house with a ¥3 million renovation condition is a ¥3 million house.

Does the municipality sell the house?

No. The town introduces; the private owner sells, directly or through a local agent. The town's conditions are separate from the sale contract.

What if I don't meet a condition after buying?

If it was tied to a subsidy, you may have to repay it. Ask for the written conditions and the penalty before applying.
