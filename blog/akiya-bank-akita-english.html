Akiya bank in Akita: cheap houses, how to search in English, snow and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Akita has no English-language akiya portal that could be confirmed. Akita Prefecture publishes a Japanese-language relocation portal that links to the akiya banks of 25 municipalities, and two national banks (At Home and LIFULL HOME'S) list houses from across Japan. So searching "cheap houses in Akita" means using browser translation and reading each municipality's own rules. The municipal pages checked for this guide did not mention nationality, but most ask that buyers intend to settle locally, and the sale itself runs through a licensed agent. Before you get excited about a low price, check three things for the exact address: winter snow cost, landslide and flood zoning, and rebuilding rights. Prices and subsidies change every year, so confirm with the municipality.

Where Akita's akiya listings are

1. Prefectural portal. Akita Prefecture's akiya page says that an akiya bank "passes on information from people who want to rent or sell a vacant house to people who want to use one," and that the prefecture publishes each municipality's listings on its relocation portal (prefectural page, last updated 19 January 2023 on the page itself). The portal's akiya page is a-iju.jp/live/akiya; when checked for this guide it linked to 25 municipalities in three regional groups (north, central, south). It did not show listing counts or conditions, which are left to each municipality.

2. National banks. The prefectural page names the nationwide akiya banks run by At Home and LIFULL HOME'S. Use them to see which towns have stock, then go to the town's own page for the rules. Our guide to akiya bank conditions explains the typical registration flow, and the Niigata and Yamanashi guides show the same approach for other prefectures.

3. Municipal pages. Search the Japanese term 秋田県 [town name] 空き家バンク. Akita City's page is a useful example of what to expect (below).

What the official pages say

Akita City's akiya bank

According to Akita City's akiya bank overview, owners register a vacant house and must have a sale or rental contract with a cooperating real-estate company; the city handles registration and information sharing, and negotiation and contracts go through the agent. Buyers or renters are expected to seek the property "for settling in Akita City." A brokerage fee set by law applies. The page states that from 1 July 2026 the city widened the eligible properties (houses, shops and offices, and in some cases damaged or dilapidated buildings). The page does not mention nationality or set a separate residency period. Other municipalities may differ, so this is an example, not a rule for the whole prefecture.

A subsidy example

For fiscal 2026, Akita City's akiya settlement subsidy page describes renovation help for houses bought or rented through the akiya bank or an agent: half of eligible work costs, capped at 1,000,000 yen for a mover from outside the city who buys, and 300,000 yen for one who rents. Conditions listed include a house at least 10 years old, living in it or renting it out for residential use for 10 years or more, and using a contractor based in the city. Applications were shown as open until 28 December 2026, but the page notes the budget can run out earlier. Other municipalities run their own programmes, and the prefectural page mentions a separate renovation subsidy for relocating households, so check each one. Subsidies are often not combinable with other aid, and the amount is not guaranteed.

Hazards: use the national map first

The Hazard Map Portal (MLIT and the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan) overlays flood, landslide, storm surge and tsunami data and links to municipal hazard maps. See our hazard map walkthrough for what the colours mean. Akita City's disaster page also says landslide zone drawings can be viewed at its disaster prevention division counter; its published zone counts there date from March 2020, which shows why counts should not be treated as current. Akita Prefecture also has a long coastline on the Sea of Japan and many rivers, so check flood depth and tsunami layers separately for the exact address. Rural lots on slopes also raise retaining-wall questions (retaining wall guide).

Snow: the cost nobody lists

Akita Prefecture maintains a Fourth Basic Plan for Heavy Snow Area Countermeasures (dated October 2023, see the prefectural page), which tells you snow is a planning issue at government level. The page did not state which municipalities are designated, so check the address's designation with the municipality. A listing photo taken in summer shows nothing about roof snow load, who clears the roof, or whether the access road is ploughed. Our guide to buying in Japan's heavy snow areas lists the questions to send. Ask also how the house is heated and whether it uses propane (propane vs city gas costs), and whether a well or septic tank could freeze (well water, jokaso guide).

Three things buyers miss in Akita

  1. Settle-in intent and subsidy strings. Akita City's rules expect settlement, and the subsidy described above requires 10 years of residential use. Leaving early may have consequences, so read our residency requirement guide and ask in writing.
  2. Rebuilding rights and land-use zoning. A cheap rural house may sit on land where rebuilding is restricted or in an urbanisation control area; see rebuilding-prohibited properties and urbanisation control areas. Houses attached to farmland also need a permit (farmland guide).
  3. Inheritance and registry gaps. Many akiya have not had ownership updated after a death. Read the registry before a deposit (registry guide, inheritance registration).

Costs and prices

No reliable Akita-wide average price was found in official sources for this guide, and rural asking prices range from very low for houses needing full repair to much higher near city centres. To see what comparable houses actually sold for, use MLIT's Real Estate Information Library, explained in our sold-price guide. Budget separately for the agent fee, taxes (overview), an inspection (inspection costs), renovation (estimates) and a winter reserve for snow removal and heating. Get written estimates before treating the asking price as the total. A "free" house is rarely free; see zero-yen akiya costs.

Checklist before you contact an Akita akiya bank

  • Note the municipality's Japanese name and find its page from the prefectural portal.
  • Ask whether foreign residents may register, and what "intent to settle" means in practice.
  • Check the exact address on the Hazard Map Portal and ask for the municipal zone drawing.
  • Ask who clears roof and road in winter, and what the winter running costs were.
  • Ask about water source, septic tank records and heating fuel.
  • Confirm road access and rebuilding rights in the registry and zoning.
  • Check whether any subsidy is still open and whether it has residency strings.
  • Book an inspection before paying a deposit.

FAQ

Is there an English version of Akita's akiya bank?

Not that could be confirmed. The prefecture lists each municipality's akiya bank on its relocation portal, and the municipal pages are in Japanese. Browser translation works for reading; confirm conditions with the municipality in writing.

Can a foreigner use an Akita akiya bank?

The Akita City akiya bank page, which was checked for this guide, does not mention nationality; it asks that buyers intend to settle in the city. Rules differ by municipality, so ask the office directly and do not assume either way.

How cheap are houses in Akita?

Asking prices vary widely with condition and location, and no reliable average was found in official sources for this guide. Check comparable sold prices in MLIT's Real Estate Information Library and budget separately for repairs, snow removal and taxes.
