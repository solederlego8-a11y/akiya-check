Akiya bank in Ehime: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Ehime Prefecture points akiya hunters to the Ehime Akiya Information Bank, which sits on its official relocation portal Ehime Ijuu Net (えひめ移住ネット). It is in Japanese, so search with browser translation and the words 空き家バンク, 売買 and 賃貸. The prefecture also runs an akiya consultation room. The relocation fund covers six cities and depends on Tokyo-area history. The hazard to understand first is landslide: Ehime had 31 deaths in the July 2018 heavy rain, and the prefecture reports 16,334 designated landslide warning zones, plus 6,198 more high-risk places that are not yet designated.

Where Ehime's akiya listings are

The prefecture-wide bank. The prefecture's page "What you can do now about akiya" names the Ehime Akiya Information Bank for people who want to buy or rent a vacant property and gives its address, e-iju.net/akiyashisetsu. The portal is Ehime's official relocation site, which also carries housing, jobs and lifestyle information. Consultation runs through the Ehime Hometown Living Support Center, with a prefecture office (089-922-4110, info@e-iju.net) and Tokyo and Osaka contacts, and "Ehime relocation concierges" who refer you to municipalities.

The consultation room. The prefecture's akiya comprehensive consultation window is run by the Architecture and Housing Section with the NPO Ehime Real Estate Consulting Association, part of the Ehime Vacant House Countermeasures Network. The akiya consultation room site offers case studies and guidance, including that inheritance registration became mandatory on 1 April 2024 and information on building condition surveys when buying an existing home. The section's phone is 089-912-2755. This is mainly aimed at owners, but the inheritance and inspection pointers apply to a buyer too: see our inheritance registration guide and inspection guide.

National banks. At Home (akiya-athome.jp) and LIFULL HOME'S also list Ehime towns that participate. A listing is an introduction; the conditions are on the municipality's page, as described in our akiya bank conditions guide.

Relocation money and subsidies

The prefecture's relocation support page lists six destination cities: Imabari, Uwajima, Niihama, Saijo, Ozu and Shikokuchuo. It applies to people who lived in or commuted to Tokyo's 23 wards before moving and who meet one of five conditions: a job on the "ano-kono Ehime" matching site, a professional-talent or pioneer matching programme, remote work for the previous employer, "related population" status set by the municipality, or approved start-up support. The page does not state amounts in its main text and does not mention nationality; the national framework on the Cabinet Office page caps the payment at ¥1,000,000 for a household and ¥600,000 for a single person, with a five-of-ten-years Tokyo residence test, and recipients repay the money if they leave early. Application is made to the destination city, and a buyer arriving from overseas would not meet the origin condition as written. The page references programme years from 2024 to 2026, so confirm the current year with the city.

For renovation and demolition subsidies, the prefecture's pages did not give amounts; the prefecture lists a subsidy system for removing specified dangerous vacant houses, and other support is municipal. Ask each town whether the house must be registered on the akiya bank and whether you must live there.

Hazards specific to Ehime

July 2018: heavy rain and debris flows

The Cabinet Office's white paper account of the July 2018 heavy rain counts 237 deaths nationwide: 115 in Hiroshima, 66 in Okayama, 31 in Ehime and 25 elsewhere. It says total rainfall between 28 June and 8 July exceeded 1,800 mm in parts of Shikoku. In Ehime, rivers flooded and debris flows struck areas such as Yoshida in Uwajima City, where water purification facilities were destroyed by landslides. For a buyer in the south-west of the prefecture, the lesson is practical: check the flood layer for river-side towns and the landslide layer for valley and hillside lots, and ask whether the house or lot was affected in 2018.

Landslide zones, designated and not yet designated

Ehime's landslide zone page reports 16,334 landslide warning zones designated as of 30 September 2025 (Reiwa 7). It also says the prefecture used detailed terrain data to extract another 6,198 places with high landslide risk that are not yet designated; they need detailed surveys before the formal process. The prefecture's reason was major debris-flow disasters in undesignated areas elsewhere in Japan. The practical point: a lot that is not in a zone today may still be a high-risk place. Check both the designated zones (municipal map pages list them by city) and the newly extracted places on the prefecture's sabo pages, then read the hazard map guide and the retaining wall guide.

Typhoons

The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991 to 2020 normals count 3.3 typhoon approaches a year for the Shikoku region, against 5.8 for the Japanese mainland (JMA). Treat roof, windows and drainage as normal inspection items.

Three things buyers miss in Ehime

  • Six cities only. The relocation fund page lists six cities, so a town elsewhere in the prefecture is outside it as written.
  • Not in a zone is not the same as safe. Thousands of additional places have been identified but not designated.
  • The consultation room is owner-oriented. It is useful for inheritance and inspection, but it will not choose a house for you.

Local items to check in Ehime

  • Land title. Boundary survey.
  • Farmland and orchards. Akiya with farmland.
  • Rebuild permission. Saikenchiku-fuka and urbanisation control areas.
  • Septic tank. Running costs.
  • Island houses. Ask about ferry access, water supply and medical care.

Checklist for an Ehime akiya

  • Search e-iju.net/akiyashisetsu, then open the town's own page for conditions.
  • Ask in writing whether foreign nationals can use the bank and any subsidy.
  • Check whether the town is one of the six relocation-fund cities and whether you meet the Tokyo-origin rule.
  • Check designated landslide zones and the newly extracted high-risk places for the address.
  • Check the flood layer for river-side lots and ask about 2018 effects.
  • Ask about build year, foundation and renovation needs (pre-1981 houses).
  • Arrange a building inspection before you commit.

FAQ

Is there a prefecture-wide akiya bank for Ehime?

Yes. Ehime Prefecture's akiya page names the Ehime Akiya Information Bank, which sits on the official relocation portal Ehime Ijuu Net (e-iju.net/akiyashisetsu/) for people who want to buy or rent vacant houses. The portal is in Japanese, and consultation is available through the Ehime Hometown Living Support Center.

Which Ehime cities does the relocation support fund cover?

The prefecture's relocation support page lists six cities: Imabari, Uwajima, Niihama, Saijo, Ozu and Shikokuchuo. It applies to people who lived in or commuted to Tokyo's 23 wards before the move and meet one of five work or activity conditions. Amounts are not stated on that page, so check the destination city.

What happened in Ehime in the July 2018 heavy rain?

The Cabinet Office white paper counts 237 deaths nationwide, including 31 in Ehime, and says total rainfall exceeded 1,800 mm in parts of Shikoku between 28 June and 8 July. In Ehime, rivers flooded and debris flows struck areas such as Yoshida in Uwajima, where water purification facilities were destroyed by landslides.

How many landslide warning zones does Ehime have?

The prefecture reports 16,334 landslide warning zones designated as of 30 September 2025 (Reiwa 7), and it has separately extracted 6,198 more high-risk places from detailed terrain data that are not yet designated and still need detailed surveys. So a lot outside a designated zone is not automatically safe.
