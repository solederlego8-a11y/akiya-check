Asbestos in an akiya: where it hides in old Japanese houses, and the survey you must arrange before renovating or demolishing

Updated 9 November 2026 · Published by the Akiya Check editorial team. General information from public sources, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

Short answer: asbestos (石綿, ishivata) was built into ordinary Japanese houses for decades — in roof slate, exterior and interior boards, insulation and, in older and larger buildings, sprayed coatings — and asbestos-containing versions of common materials were manufactured into the early 2000s. Left intact and undisturbed, these materials are mainly a problem for the day you renovate or demolish. That day is now regulated: under the Air Pollution Control Act, demolition and renovation work needs a pre-work asbestos survey, done by a qualified surveyor, and for larger jobs the result must be reported to the prefecture before work starts. For a buyer, the practical move is to price this in before you sign, not discover it in the first renovation quote. This is general information, not legal or health advice.

30-second decision

  • You plan to live in the house largely as-is: asbestos is a "know and don't disturb" item. Note where suspect materials are, and do not cut or drill them.
  • You plan a real renovation or demolition: a survey by a qualified surveyor is a required first step, and removal — if anything is found — is specialist work that belongs in your budget from day one. See renovation estimates and demolition costs.
  • The house is pre-1981 or has grey slate roofing and old board siding: treat asbestos as likely until a survey says otherwise, and let that feed your offer price.

Where asbestos hides in a Japanese house

The hard part is that asbestos-containing and asbestos-free versions of the same product look alike. The main places it turns up in houses:

MaterialWhere you find itEra to be suspicious of
Asbestos-cement slate (スレート) and formed boardsRoofing tiles, exterior siding, interior wall and ceiling boards, eavesVery common for decades; asbestos-containing versions were made into the early 2000s
Insulation and fireproofing materialsAround boilers, baths and flues; some wall and ceiling insulation boardsOlder houses, especially with later equipment replacements — mixed eras are normal
Sprayed asbestos (吹付け石綿)Sprayed coatings on beams, ceilings and parking or plant areas — more typical of larger or older buildings than small wooden housesMainly a pre-1990 concern; spraying was restricted in stages from the 1970s
Floor and roof materialsSome vinyl floor tiles and sheets, roofing feltOlder installations; check before ripping floors up

Manufacture of asbestos products was phased out in stages and effectively ended in the mid-2000s (products containing more than 0.1% asbestos were banned in 2006). A house built or re-roofed after that is far less likely to contain these materials — but renovations mix eras, so the build year of the house alone never settles the question.

Intact is low-risk; cutting is the problem

Asbestos harms health when fibres become airborne and are breathed in — which happens when material is cut, drilled, sanded, broken or demolished, not when a sound board sits on a wall. Two consequences for owners. First, do not treat suspect boards as a DIY material: no sawing, drilling or snapping them to "check". Second, a home inspection (see home inspections) can flag suspect materials, but confirming asbestos requires the regulated survey below, sometimes with laboratory analysis of samples.

The law now: survey first, and report the bigger jobs

The asbestos rules under the Air Pollution Control Act were strengthened in stages from 2021. In plain terms, per the Ministry of the Environment and prefectural guidance:

  • Regulated materials widened (from 1 April 2021): all asbestos-containing building materials are now within the rules, including the formed boards (so-called level-3 materials) common in houses.
  • Pre-work survey (事前調査): before demolition or renovation work, the contractor must survey whether asbestos-containing materials are present, using documents and visual inspection, with analysis where that cannot settle it. The survey records, and the written explanation given to the client, must be kept for three years after the work ends.
  • Qualified surveyors (from 1 October 2023): for work starting on or after that date, the survey must be carried out by a qualified person, such as a certified asbestos building-materials surveyor (建築物石綿含有建材調査者).
  • Reporting to the prefecture (from 1 April 2022): the main contractor must report the survey result before work starts — whether or not asbestos was found — when the job is a demolition involving 80 m² or more of floor area, or a renovation with a contract value of ¥1,000,000 or more including tax.

If asbestos is found, removal follows set work standards, with the strictest controls for sprayed asbestos and insulation, and the waste must be disposed of as specially controlled industrial waste. All of this is contractor territory: your job as buyer is to make sure the survey happens and the cost is in the quote.

FAQ

Does every old akiya contain asbestos?

No — but asbestos-containing versions of very common materials (roof slate, siding and interior boards, insulation) were manufactured into the early 2000s, and they look like the non-asbestos versions. Only a survey — documents and visual inspection, with laboratory analysis where needed — can confirm a specific house.

Is a survey legally required before I renovate?

A pre-work survey is required before demolition or renovation work, and since work starting on or after 1 October 2023 it must be carried out by a qualified asbestos building-materials surveyor. The result must be reported to the prefecture before work starts when the job is a demolition of 80 m² or more of floor area, or a renovation with a contract value of ¥1,000,000 or more including tax.

Can I just remove old boards myself?

Treat that as a professional job. The danger is fibres released when material is cut, drilled or broken. Work on asbestos-containing materials is regulated, with stricter controls for sprayed asbestos and insulation, and removal waste must be disposed of properly. Get quotes from contractors who handle asbestos work.

Who pays for the survey and removal — buyer or seller?

In a typical as-is akiya sale, they become the buyer's project costs after purchase. That is why the check belongs before you sign: a likely survey and removal bill is a legitimate input to your offer price and your renovation budget.

Details of surveys and reporting are handled by contractors and prefectures; confirm for your project.
