Akiya buying checks in order: which to do first, and when to walk away

Updated 13 October 2026 · Published by the Akiya Check editorial team. General information from public sources, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

Short answer: check the things that can kill the deal for free or near-free first (can you buy, can you rebuild, who owns it, does it flood), and spend money on inspections and surveys only for the house that survives those. Most guides explain each check separately; this one puts them in the order that avoids wasted deposits and lists the answer at each step that should make you stop. It links to the detailed guide for every step. It is general information from public sources, not legal, tax or brokerage advice.

30-second decision

  • You have a listing but nothing else: do steps 1 to 4 below before you contact the agent about price.
  • You are about to pay a deposit: make sure steps 1 to 7 are answered in writing; the deposit usually cannot be recovered after signing (see step 8).
  • You are deciding between listings: run steps 2 to 4 on each and compare the answers; the Compare 3 report is built for that.

How to use the table

  1. Start with the free ones. Steps 1 to 4 mostly need public maps, the listing and a registry certificate (a few hundred yen; see the registry guide). They can be done in an evening, before any agent time or fee.
  2. Write the questions down. The free 12-point checklist turns steps 2 to 7 into questions you can send the agent.
  3. Pay for the survey only on a survivor. A building inspection or a boundary survey costs real money; do it on the one house that passed steps 1 to 5.
  4. Do not skip step 8. The deposit is the point of no return; see the deposit guide.

FAQ

Which check is the most likely to end a deal?

We have no statistics to rank them. Rebuilding restrictions, ownership problems and hazard zones are the checks where a "no" cannot be fixed by a repair, so do them first.

Do I need all nine checks for every house?

No. Skip the ones that clearly do not apply (for example farmland if there is none), but note that you skipped them and why.

Can I do these checks without speaking Japanese?

Many of the sources are in Japanese. The guides explain the terms, and the checklist gives you the questions; a translator or the agent can help with documents.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is general information. For a specific house, confirm with the agent, the municipality and licensed professionals.
