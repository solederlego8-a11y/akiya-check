Akiya Check vs. Japan Property Research: which one do you need?

Short answer: if you are looking at one specific listing and want a plain-English verdict within a day, Akiya Check is built for that. If you are researching many properties over months — as an agent, investor, or team — a map-and-database subscription like Japan Property Research is built for that instead. This page compares them factually, using Japan Property Research's own public pricing and feature pages (checked September 2026). We are not affiliated with Japan Property Research; if anything here is out of date, their pricing page is the source of truth.

What each product actually is

Japan Property Research is a self-serve web platform: you log in, search a lot number or address, and view map layers — zoning, official hazard designations, land price history, and a registry (touki) lookup — across a claimed 1.2M+ listings and 102M+ lots nationwide. Paid tiers add "Market Watch," "Market Comp," exports, alerts, and short-term rental analytics. Plans are billed monthly with a 7-day free trial, and the Business tier is explicitly built for teams ("3 seats," "shared workspace").

Akiya Check is a done-for-you report: you send one listing URL (or paste the listing text), and within 24 hours you receive an English PDF that combines the same category of official government data (zoning, hazards, population trend, nearby transactions) with something a map cannot do — a reading of the actual listing text for Japan-specific risk facts (rebuild restrictions, septic vs. sewer, leasehold, disclosure matters, akiya-bank conditions), scored and ranked by severity, plus a ready-to-send bilingual question list for the agent.

When Japan Property Research is the better fit

  • You are screening dozens of properties across regions and want to browse zoning/hazard layers yourself
  • You need land price history, market comps, or short-term rental analytics on an ongoing basis
  • You are a team that wants a shared workspace and pooled research history
  • You are comfortable interpreting map layers and cross-referencing them against a listing yourself

When Akiya Check is the better fit

  • You have one listing (or a short list of two or three) you are seriously considering right now
  • You do not read Japanese and want the listing's own red-flag terms (再建築不可, 浄化槽, 借地権, 告知事項) explained, not just the surrounding map data
  • You would rather pay once than start a monthly subscription for a one-time decision
  • You want a ready-made, bilingual list of questions to send the agent today, not a tool to learn

FAQ

Is Akiya Check a competitor or an alternative to Japan Property Research?
They solve different problems. Japan Property Research is a self-serve map and database subscription for browsing zoning, hazard and price layers yourself. Akiya Check is a done-for-you report for one specific listing you are already considering. Many buyers could reasonably use both, or neither, depending on how many properties they are evaluating.

Which one is cheaper?
For a single property, Akiya Check's one-time $49 fee is normally cheaper than a Japan Property Research monthly subscription, since the subscription keeps billing until you cancel. For someone evaluating dozens of properties over months, a subscription may work out cheaper per look.

Does Japan Property Research read the actual listing text?
Based on its published features, Japan Property Research's core product is map layers (lot boundaries, zoning, hazards, land price, registry lookup) plus market analytics. We did not find a feature that parses an individual listing's text for facts such as rebuild restrictions, septic tanks, leasehold status, or disclosure matters — that is the core of what an Akiya Check report does.
