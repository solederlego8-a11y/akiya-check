Kokuchi jiko (告知事項): what "disclosure matters" means on a Japanese listing, what must be disclosed, and how much it should change the price

Short answer: 告知事項 (kokuchi jiko, "matters to be disclosed") is a flag that the seller or agent has something they are legally obliged to tell you before you sign. Most often it is a psychological defect — a death in the property — but the same phrase covers physical, environmental and legal defects. It is not automatically a reason to walk away; it is a reason to ask exactly what the matter is, get it in writing, and price it.

What does 告知事項あり actually mean?

In Japanese real-estate practice, the phrase 告知事項あり ("there are disclosure matters") or 心理的瑕疵あり ("there is a psychological defect") appears on a listing when the agent knows of a fact that a reasonable buyer would want to know and that is not visible from an inspection. Under the Real Estate Brokerage Act (宅地建物取引業法), the licensed agent must explain such facts in the 重要事項説明 (jūyō jikō setsumei, "explanation of important matters") before the contract, and deliberately hiding them exposes the seller to liability for non-conformity (契約不適合責任) under the Civil Code.

The four categories buyers meet in practice:

CategoryJapaneseTypical examples on an akiya
Psychological心理的瑕疵Suicide, homicide, fire death or a death discovered late (孤独死) in the house; a nearby incident that is widely known
Physical物理的瑕疵Termite damage, roof leaks, soil contamination, buried objects, asbestos in older materials
Environmental環境的瑕疵Odour or noise from a nearby plant, a waste facility, a religious facility, a planned road
Legal法的瑕疵Rebuilding prohibited (再建築不可), urbanization control area, illegal extensions, a road setback, leasehold land

Legal defects are the most common on very cheap houses and are covered in our guides on saikenchiku fuka and urbanization control areas. This page focuses on the psychological category, because that is what agents usually mean when they write 告知事項あり without further detail.

What does the law actually require the agent to disclose about a death?

Until 2021 there was no written standard, and agents disclosed (or didn't) case by case. In October 2021 the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) published the Guideline on disclosure of deaths by real-estate brokers (宅地建物取引業者による人の死の告知に関するガイドライン). Its core rules, in plain terms:

  • Natural deaths and accidental deaths in daily life (illness, old age, a fall at home) in principle do not need to be disclosed.
  • Suicide, homicide, fire deaths and other non-natural deaths in the property or its common areas do need to be disclosed, as does a natural death that was discovered late enough to require special cleaning (特殊清掃).
  • For rentals, the duty to disclose lapses roughly three years after the event. For sales the guideline sets no time limit: if the agent knows, they should tell a buyer.
  • If the buyer asks directly, the agent must answer truthfully regardless of the category or the time elapsed.

Source: MLIT, 宅地建物取引業者による人の死の告知に関するガイドライン (2021). The guideline is administrative guidance rather than a statute, but courts and the industry treat it as the standard of care.

The practical consequence for akiya buyers: many old houses are on the market precisely because the last occupant died there. A death from old age, even inside the house, normally will not appear as 告知事項. When the flag does appear, it is usually the non-natural kind, or the agent is being cautious.

How do I find out what the disclosure matter is before I commit?

The listing will rarely say. Ask in writing, before any deposit, and keep the reply. Three questions, in Japanese and English:

Question (JP)MeaningWhat a good answer contains
告知事項の内容を具体的に教えてください。Please tell me specifically what the disclosure matter is.The category (death / physical / environmental / legal), when it happened, and where (which room, common area, next door)
人の死に関する事項であれば、経緯・時期・特殊清掃の有無を教えてください。If it concerns a death: circumstances, date, and whether special cleaning was done.Whether it was natural or not; year; whether the interior was replaced
重要事項説明書にその内容を記載していただけますか。Will you write it into the explanation of important matters?"Yes" — a refusal is itself a red flag

Agents are generally willing to explain by phone or email once asked directly. If the answer is "the seller prefers not to say", treat the matter as unknown and price accordingly, or pass.

How much should a psychological defect change the price?

There is no statutory discount. Market practice, as described in Japanese appraisal literature and agent commentary, is roughly:

SituationTypical market treatment
Natural death, discovered promptlyOften no discount; frequently not even disclosed under the guideline
Late-discovered natural death with special cleaningModest discount, commonly cited in the range of 10–20% off comparable value
Suicide inside the propertyLarger discount, commonly cited around 20–30%; interior often fully replaced
Homicide or a widely reported incident30–50% or more; some buyers exclude these entirely, some investors seek them out

These ranges are rules of thumb, not measurements, and for a ¥2 million akiya the absolute amount is small. The larger cost is usually resale liquidity: you will have to disclose the same matter when you sell, and the pool of buyers shrinks. If you plan to demolish and rebuild, the psychological defect attaches to the land, not the building, but in practice buyers care less once the original structure is gone.

Is a death next door, or in the apartment building, a disclosure matter?

The guideline covers the property itself and the common areas a buyer would normally use (entrance, corridors, stairs). An incident in a neighbouring detached house is not, strictly, the agent's duty to disclose — but if it is widely known locally and would affect a reasonable buyer's decision, careful agents mention it, and you can ask. For a detached akiya, "next door" is usually outside the duty; for an apartment (マンション), a death in the shared lift or entrance is inside it.

What about the other kinds of 告知事項?

Physical and legal defects are where the money actually goes on old houses. If the agent's answer to "what is the matter?" is one of the following, budget for it before you decide:

  • Termite or rot (シロアリ・腐食): ask for the inspection report; treatment and repair can run from tens of thousands to several million yen depending on structural damage.
  • Leaks (雨漏り): ask where and how long; a roof replacement on a small house is typically a seven-figure yen job.
  • Asbestos (アスベスト): common in roofing and boards of houses built before the mid-2000s; only a cost if you remove or renovate, but then a licensed contractor is required.
  • Illegal extensions (違法増築): may block financing and future permits; see our guide on rebuilding restrictions.
  • Septic tank (浄化槽): not a defect as such, but a recurring cost that agents sometimes list here; see the jokaso guide.

Checklist: what to have in writing before signing

  • The specific content of every 告知事項, written into the 重要事項説明書
  • For a death: category, year, location in the property, and whether special cleaning or interior replacement was done
  • For physical defects: any inspection report (建物状況調査) and repair estimates
  • Confirmation of whether the seller is exempt from non-conformity liability (契約不適合責任の免責) — common on cheap akiya sold by individuals, which means the disclosure is your only protection
  • Your own written question and the agent's written answer, kept with the contract

FAQ

Does 告知事項あり always mean someone died there?

No. It means there is something the agent must disclose. Deaths are the most common reason for the bare phrase, but physical, environmental and legal defects use the same wording. Ask which.

Can the seller refuse to tell me what happened?

The seller can be reluctant, but the licensed agent has a legal duty to disclose known facts that affect the decision, and must answer a direct question truthfully. A refusal is a reason to walk away or to price the property as if the worst case were true.

If I buy and later discover an undisclosed death, what can I do?

If the seller or agent knew and did not disclose, you may have a claim for non-conformity (contract termination or damages) and the agent may face administrative sanction. The claim is harder if the seller genuinely did not know. Keep every written exchange.

Do I have to disclose it when I resell?

For sales, yes — the MLIT guideline sets no expiry for known non-natural deaths. Plan for a smaller buyer pool and a lower price when you exit.
