Can you Airbnb an akiya? Japan's minpaku rules: the 180-day cap, the notification, and what to check before you buy

Updated 9 November 2026 · Published by the Akiya Check editorial team. General information from public sources, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

Short answer: yes, a foreign owner can legally let a Japanese house to paying guests — but never just by opening a platform account. Paid lodging in a house runs under one of three regimes, and the easiest one, a notification under the Private Lodging Business Act (住宅宿泊事業法, the "minpaku law", in force since 15 June 2018), caps you at 180 letting days a year. If you live abroad, you must also hire a government-registered manager, and your municipality can restrict or effectively block minpaku in some zones by ordinance. All of that is knowable before you buy — and it is much cheaper to check then. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

30-second decision

  • You want occasional income while you use the house yourself: the minpaku notification route is designed for that — if the local ordinance allows it and you can line up a registered manager.
  • You are counting on year-round rental income to make the purchase work: the 180-day cap breaks that plan. You would need an Inns and Hotels Act licence, which an old house may not qualify for. Re-run your numbers before buying.
  • The house is on an akiya bank or in a restricted zone: check the bank's conditions and the zoning first — some listings cannot be used commercially at all. See akiya-bank conditions and urbanization control areas.

The three legal routes to paid lodging

Charging a guest to sleep in a house is, by default, business under the Inns and Hotels Act (旅館業法), which needs a licence. Two later regimes created alternatives:

Letting with neither a notification nor a licence is unlicensed business under the Inns and Hotels Act. Platforms do not fix this for you: the legal duty sits with the host.

What the minpaku route requires

  • A qualifying "dwelling" (住宅): the house must have a kitchen, bathroom, toilet and washstand facilities, and fall into one of the Act's occupancy categories — a house someone currently lives in, a house being offered for tenants, or a house the owner uses from time to time, such as a second home.
  • A notification for each house, filed before you start, through the Japan Tourism Agency's minpaku system. Municipal guidance states the attachments include a fire-service compliance notice (消防法令適合通知書), so expect to deal with the local fire department about alarms and equipment — see fire insurance and fire safety for the house side of this.
  • Ongoing duties: display the notification sign (標識), keep a guest register, explain house rules to guests, handle complaints, and report your letting days and guest numbers to the prefecture on a regular cycle.

The 180-day cap, and how it is counted

The cap is not a calendar-year allowance. The year runs from noon on 1 April to noon on the following 1 April, and one "day" runs noon to noon. Even a fully booked house earns at most half a year of nights under this route. If your purchase case needs more nights than that, the minpaku route is the wrong tool — see the licence row in the table above, and treat any seller's or agent's income projection that quietly assumes 365 nights as a red flag.

If you live abroad: the registered-manager rule

Minpaku hosts fall into two types. A resident host lives in the house while guests stay. An absentee host — which includes almost every overseas owner — must entrust the management work (住宅宿泊管理業務) to a property manager registered with the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (住宅宿泊管理業者). The same duty applies if you let more than five rooms. In practice: before you buy, confirm that a registered manager actually covers that municipality and get their fee in writing. Rural areas can have few or none, which is a purchase-stopping fact you want early. The general problem of running a house from abroad is covered in managing an akiya from overseas.

Local ordinances can cut it further — or shut it down

Article 18 of the Act lets prefectures and municipalities restrict minpaku by ordinance, setting zones and periods where it may not operate, to protect the local living environment. Some cities restrict letting on weekdays near schools, or in quiet residential zones for much of the year, and municipalities have been tightening these rules. The national law gives you a ceiling of 180 days; the ordinance for the exact address tells you what you really get. Read it before you buy, not after.

FAQ

Can I rent out an akiya on Airbnb without any paperwork?

No. Charging guests to stay requires either a notification under the Private Lodging Business Act (minpaku) or a licence under the Inns and Hotels Act. Letting without either is unlicensed business under the Inns and Hotels Act.

How many nights a year can I let under the minpaku notification?

Up to 180 days per year, counted from noon on 1 April to noon on the following 1 April. A municipality may restrict the zones or periods further by ordinance, so your usable days can be fewer.

I live overseas. Can I still run a minpaku in Japan?

Yes, but as an absentee host you must entrust the management work to a property manager registered with the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (a 住宅宿泊管理業者). Budget for that contract before you buy.

How do I let for more than 180 days?

Only under a different regime: a licence under the Inns and Hotels Act (for example a simple-lodging licence), which has facility and management standards, or special-zone minpaku where a municipality offers it. Neither is automatic for an old house.

Sources: Private Lodging Business Act (住宅宿泊事業法, Act No. 65 of 2017, in force 15 June 2018), including the 180-day limit, the notification duty and the Article 18 ordinance power; Japan Tourism Agency, Minpaku portal site (notification system, registered managers, day-count rules); Aichi Prefecture, minpaku notification guidance. Ordinances and fire-service requirements differ by municipality; confirm for the specific address.
