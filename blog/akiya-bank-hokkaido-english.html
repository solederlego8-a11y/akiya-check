Akiya bank in Hokkaido: how to search it in English, relocation subsidies and hazard checks (2026)

Short answer: Hokkaido has the one thing many prefectures lack: a single prefecture-wide akiya bank, hokkaido-akiya.com, running since April 2016 under the prefecture's housing division. It is Japanese-only, so you search with browser translation and the Japanese words 空き家バンク, 売買 (sale) and 賃貸 (rental). Money for newcomers is set town by town, and the figures in the prefecture's own directory range from a few hundred thousand yen to ¥1,900,000. The hazard picture differs from the rest of Japan because winter dominates it: snow load on empty roofs, frozen pipes, and a Cabinet Office tsunami estimate that is worst for a winter night. Large plots near designated facilities can also trigger a pre-contract notification under the Important Land Survey Act.

Where to search: Hokkaido's akiya listings

The prefectural akiya bank. The Hokkaido Akiya Information Bank (北海道空き家情報バンク) is described on the prefecture's vacant-house measures page as part of its response to vacant housing; the Housing Division can be reached on 011-204-5576. The search site at hokkaido-akiya.com connects owners who want to sell or rent with prospective residents, and daily operation is handled by the Hokkaido Real Estate Transaction Council (北海道宅地建物取引業協会). You can browse all listings, use a filtered search, or save favourites. The site carries a notice that Hokkaido does not guarantee the accuracy, usefulness or safety of the information it lists, which is a reminder that a listing here is not a warranty. The pages reviewed show no English or other-language option.

The relocation portal. Kuraso Hokkaido (北海道で暮らそう), run by the Hokkaido Migration and Exchange Promotion Council, has housing information, a municipality search, a relocation and living-cost simulator and one-stop consultation windows for municipalities. The prefecture's Hokkaido Furusato Relocation Promotion Center has a Tokyo window and a Sapporo window with online consultation by advance booking. Both are in Japanese; if you need a translator, bring one.

The national akiya banks. At Home's nationwide akiya bank has a page for Hokkaido at akiya-athome.jp/buy/01/ (and /rent/01/ for rentals); "01" is the prefecture code. LIFULL HOME'S runs the other national akiya bank. Use these to see which municipalities have stock, then go to the town's own page for conditions. The general rules about who may apply are in our akiya bank conditions guide.

Searching Hokkaido in English: practical tips

  • Search the Japanese place name. Browser translation turns town names into guesses. Copy 札幌市, 函館市, 旭川市, 倶知安町, 小樽市 and the like exactly as written.
  • Three terms: 北海道 空き家バンク, then 売買 for sale or 賃貸 for rental, then the town name in front for local conditions.
  • Distances are large. Check the road distance to the nearest hospital, supermarket and snow-clearing service, not the straight-line distance on the map.
  • Land area matters twice. Check both the land area (土地) and the building floor area (建物); a lot or farmhouse of 200 square metres or more matters for the land-notification rule below.
  • Use the red-flag glossary. Terms like 再建築不可, 市街化調整区域, 借地 and 農地 are explained in our kanji red-flag guide.

Relocation and renovation money in Hokkaido

Relocation support fund

The prefecture's special page (移住支援金特設ページ) explains that the national, prefectural and municipal governments jointly pay ¥1,000,000 (¥600,000 for a single person) to people who move from Tokyo's 23 wards, or commute there, to a participating Hokkaido municipality and work for a company on the prefecture's matching site. Applications must be made within one year of moving in. The list of participating municipalities is published by the prefecture. The Cabinet Office's national page adds the five-year residence intention and the repayment rule if you leave within five years. Neither page mentions foreign nationals, and the Tokyo-origin requirement means that someone moving from overseas would not meet it as written.

Town-by-town housing subsidies

The prefecture also maintains a directory of municipal support for people moving to Hokkaido, arranged by sub-prefecture. It shows how much local rules differ. Chippubetsu Town lists renovation support of one-third of eligible costs up to ¥300,000 for an owned home, and rental help of up to ¥25,000 a month. Akkeshi Town lists 10 percent of the work cost up to ¥200,000. Rankoshi Town is listed with amounts up to ¥1,900,000 for comprehensive improvements. These are headline figures copied from a directory page: whether a given programme requires the house to be on the town's akiya bank, how long you must stay and whether foreign residents may apply are questions for the town, and budgets change each year.

Hazards to check in Hokkaido

Winter makes every other hazard worse

In December 2021 the Cabinet Office published damage estimates for large earthquakes along the Japan Trench and the Kuril Trench (explanatory page). Two points are directly relevant to a Hokkaido buyer. First, the highest death toll in the estimate arises on a winter night, when evacuation starts late and snow makes movement difficult: about 199,000 for the Japan Trench case and about 100,000 for the Kuril Trench case. Second, the estimates add a separate count of people at risk of hypothermia who survive the tsunami: about 42,000 and 22,000 respectively. Building damage is also highest on a winter evening, because snow raises structural damage and fire spread. These are maximum-class scenarios used for planning, not forecasts, but they explain why a coastal Hokkaido address should be checked for the tsunami layer on the national hazard map and for the walking route to high ground in deep snow.

Roof snow in an empty house

An occupied house gets its roof cleared or sheds snow by heating; an empty one does not. The snow-disaster site of the National Research Institute for Earth Science and Disaster Resilience (seppyo.bosai.go.jp) hosts a Hokkaido roof-snow tool called 雪おろシグナル that tracks conditions over the last 24 hours and 30 days and links to a snow-load calculation page; it shows how seriously roof snow weight is treated in the region. For a buyer, the practical questions are written, not theoretical: Who will clear the roof while you are abroad? Is there a local company on contract? Is there a snow-guard system, and does the roof shed snow onto a neighbour's land or a public road? Our home inspection guide explains how a building inspection checks roof structure and the signs of past leaks.

Ground: fill land and liquefaction

The September 2018 Hokkaido Eastern Iburi earthquake produced liquefaction and subsidence on filled valley land in parts of Sapporo (reported in MLIT and academic damage reports; see for instance this MLIT document, in Japanese). If you are looking at a post-war suburban house in Sapporo or another city, read our liquefaction guide and check the large-scale fill land map before the price.

Cold-weather checks specific to Hokkaido

Frozen pipes. A guidance note written for international students in Sapporo (Hokkaido University; not a municipal publication) states that you should drain the water pipes when the outside temperature falls below minus 4 degrees Celsius, and whenever you are away from home for more than a day, using the 水抜き (mizunuki) valve. For a house left empty for months, ask the municipal waterworks what they recommend and whether the water can be shut off at the meter. Also ask what happened last winter: burst pipes are a prime source of hidden damage.

Heating and fuel. Ask what heats the house and what it costs per winter, and where the fuel tank is. A listing that says 灯油 (kerosene) or プロパン (propane) is telling you about an ongoing cost; the jokaso guide covers the septic side of rural running costs.

Insulation and structure. Ask for the original drawings, and about insulation and window type; an older house without insulation may be cold and expensive to heat. If it dates from before 1981, see the pre-1981 guide.

One land-purchase rule to check: the Important Land Survey Act

Under the Important Land Survey Act (重要土地等調査法), the Cabinet Office designates "special attention areas" (特別注視区域): areas within roughly 1,000 metres of important facilities such as defence-related sites, and areas on border islands. For a sale or other transfer of land or buildings inside such an area where the land area, or the total floor area of a building, is 200 square metres or more, both the seller and the buyer must notify the Prime Minister before the contract is signed. Properties in the wider "attention areas" are not subject to the pre-contract notification. The Cabinet Office's area page points to an interactive web map (resum2.go.jp) where you can search an address or lot number to see whether it is inside a designated area. The pages reviewed describe the duty as applying to the contracting parties and do not make it depend on nationality. Whether a Hokkaido lot is affected depends entirely on where it is, so ask the agent to check, early, because it affects the timing of the contract. The wider legal context is in our guide on whether foreigners can buy an akiya.

Checklist for a Hokkaido akiya

  • Find the town's own akiya bank page and read its conditions; the prefecture-wide site is only the shop window.
  • Look up the address for flooding, landslide and tsunami on the national hazard map, and plan the winter evacuation route.
  • Ask who clears roof snow while you are away, and get a quote.
  • Ask how the pipes were drained last winter and how the house is heated, with last year's fuel cost.
  • Check the address on the Cabinet Office's land web map and note whether the land or building exceeds 200 square metres.
  • Ask the town in writing whether foreign residents may use its subsidies and akiya bank.
  • Have a building inspection done before you commit.

FAQ

Does Hokkaido have an English akiya bank?

Hokkaido operates a prefecture-wide akiya bank at hokkaido-akiya.com, but the pages reviewed for this guide show no English or other-language option. Use browser translation with the Japanese terms 空き家バンク, 売買 and 賃貸, and confirm details with the agent or municipality.

Do I need to notify the government when buying land in Hokkaido?

Only if the property lies in a designated special attention area under the Important Land Survey Act and the land or building has an area of 200 square metres or more. In that case the Cabinet Office says both seller and buyer must notify the Prime Minister before the contract is concluded. The Cabinet Office's web map lets you search an address to see whether it is inside a designated area.

How do I stop water pipes freezing in an empty Hokkaido house?

Guidance written for Sapporo residents advises draining the water (水抜き) when the outside temperature falls below minus 4 degrees Celsius and whenever you leave the house for more than a day. Your municipal waterworks and a local plumber can tell you how it works for the specific house.

Are Hokkaido renovation subsidies large?

They vary widely by town. The prefecture's directory of municipal programs lists examples such as one-third of renovation costs up to ¥300,000 in one town and 10 percent up to ¥200,000 in another, while a third municipality is listed with amounts up to ¥1,900,000. Check each town's current rules, since budgets are set yearly.

Is winter the biggest risk for a Hokkaido house?

For the building, snow load and frozen pipes are the practical risks in a house left empty. For disasters, the Cabinet Office's estimate for Japan Trench and Kuril Trench earthquakes says deaths would be highest in a winter night, when snow slows evacuation and cold adds a hypothermia risk.
